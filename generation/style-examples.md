# Short manual style references

Original fictional examples for tone only. Not roster facts, source evidence or episodes to copy. The names and props below are generic; use the actual supplied cast and a different situation in generated documents.

## Conversational, not a series of speeches

Maker: The first version looked better. Everyone liked the brass handle.
Shopkeeper: Everyone who didn't have to clean around it.
Maker: Fair. We kept the plain one. I can remove it with the same tool I use on the shelves.
Shopkeeper: He brought the shiny one back twice, hoping I'd change my mind.
Maker: Once. The second time I was bringing biscuits.
Host: I'm recording that as a disputed biscuit visit. What actually changed for the shop?
Shopkeeper: I can take the shelf out myself now. That's the useful bit.

## Local reporting that doesn't oversell

The repair counter is keeping its old wooden sign. What changed this week is underneath it: a narrow shelf where customers can put a bag while trying on their boots.

The owner said the idea came from watching people balance parcels against the door. A neighbor cut the shelf from an offcut; the owner fitted it after closing. Neither described it as an innovation project. The owner did say customers had stopped handing her their shopping while she was holding a shoe.

## Social voices can be brief

Alteration worker: A pocket is allowed to be useful. You can put the decorative flap somewhere else.
Customer: Even on a party coat?
Alteration worker: Especially on a party coat. Where were you planning to keep your return ticket?

## A manager's note needn't sound like a legal report

Asked the shopkeeper about the new counter shelf. She says she and a neighbor fitted it after closing; I haven't spoken with him. She laughed about the first customer asking whether it was a display for hats. She might add a second shelf, but wants to see whether this one gets in the way on market day first.

## Variety rule

Use these examples for economy, ordinary physical detail, distinct voices and modest humor. Do not copy their incidents, shelf, brass handle, biscuits, or return-ticket joke. A collection should also contain serious civic questions, sensible disagreement, quiet satisfaction and straightforward practical news. Not every artifact needs a correction, failure or punchline.

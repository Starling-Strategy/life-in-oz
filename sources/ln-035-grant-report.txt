FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE
April 2024
Grant funds from the Emerald City Leadership Network’s Small Business Circle were directed toward two member-led initiatives in the first quarter of 2024: a skills-sharing project in Yellow Kiln and a planned equipment upgrade to support regional events.

Aurelia Quadwood of Quadwood's Barbers used her portion of the grant to purchase professional-grade combs, shears, and instructional materials. Over three consecutive weekends, she taught basic hair-cutting techniques to a local teenager from Yellow Kiln.

Aurelia Quadwood:
The kid showed real interest, so we started with the fundamentals—how to hold the tools, how to section hair, straight lines on a mannequin. By the third weekend, they were ready to try a simple trim on a willing brother. I sent them home with a starter kit so they could keep practicing on family. It’s not about making a barber out of them in a month, it’s about giving them a skill they can always fall back on.

The remaining funds are supporting Lux Thistlemore of Thistlemore Catering in a research phase. Lux is investigating the purchase of durable, fuel-efficient chafing dishes specifically for rental to caterers and community groups hosting outdoor events in Gillikin Country.

Lux Thistlemore:
Gillikin garden weddings are beautiful, but the logistics are a challenge. You need equipment that holds heat steadily in the open air without guzzling fuel cans all evening. I’m comparing materials, insulation, and burner types. The goal is to have a small, reliable set available for rent by next season—it’ll cut down on waste and transport costs for a lot of us.

Grant money has been fully allocated. Aurelia’s project is complete, with the tools purchased and the instruction given. Lux’s research is ongoing; no equipment has been purchased yet. The Small Business Circle will review outcomes and usage later this year.

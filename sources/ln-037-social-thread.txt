Festival Committee Thread: Logistics, Sound, and Tuba Mutes

SYNTHETIC SOURCE — original fictional text; no real event or publication.
Format: social_thread
Theme: Festival Committee

Wynn Fernshaw:
Hello all. Wynn Fernshaw here. I’m finalizing the fabric order for the community pageant banners and was thinking about storage for the spools. Before I get into that, a quick, unrelated note: a teacher from the Blue Orchard school stopped me at the market. She mentioned the students used that box of scrap fabric I’d passed along for their collage and puppet-making unit. She said they were quite taken with the textures, especially the heavier brocade pieces. It was nice to hear.

Milo Plumton:
Wynn! That’s lovely. Textures are everything. Speaking of things getting misplaced… I, Milo Plumton, offer a cautionary tale for our equipment lists. Last month, for the Munchkin Founders’ Day parade warm-up, we were all set when we realized the soloist’s specialized tuba mute was missing. A frantic twenty-minute search ensued. It had rolled clear across the bandstand and lodged itself under the percussion cart. We found it, slightly dented, but the delay meant we started the warm-up with cold lips. Not ideal. I’m now advocating for a ‘mute check’ as part of our pre-event roll call.

Yves Rushford:
Yves Rushford here. I can sympathize, Milo. Last-minute faults are the worst. I was volunteering at my niece’s primary school play in Gate Quarter last week. During the final dress rehearsal, the main speaker feed cut out—just a faint buzz from the back. I traced it to a faulty connector on the main cable; the solder joint had cracked. Had to run home for my kit, re-solder it, and test the line. Got it done before the curtain. The important part was the kids’ voices carried to the back row. A simple fix, but it reminds me we should budget time for a full cable inspection for any of our outdoor festival stages.

Isla Poppywood:
Isla Poppywood. That’s good preventative work, Yves. My mind runs to contingencies. I’ve been drafting a simple set of check-call protocols for drivers—relevant for any festival where we’re moving gear across country. Based on getting stranded once in a sudden Winkie Country dust storm. The proposal is: if visibility drops below a certain point, you pull over safely, call in your location and status to dispatch or a designated contact, then wait for the all-clear. No trying to push through. It’s about making sure people and cargo are accounted for. I’d like to propose we adopt a version of this for our long-haul volunteer drivers during the festival load-in/out.

Wynn Fernshaw:
A protocol sounds wise, Isla. It connects to my original point about storage, in a way. Knowing where things are—fabric, mutes, cables, people—is half the battle. Milo, would a designated ‘small items’ crate on the equipment cart help? Yves, for the cable check, do you need a second pair of hands? I’m quite good with a needle; I can manage a spool of solder.

Yves Rushford:
An extra set of eyes and steady hands is always welcome, Wynn. Thank you. Isla, I’d like to see that protocol draft. It sounds solid.

Milo Plumton:
A dedicated crate is a brilliant idea, Wynn. I’ll bring it up with the band manager. And Isla, yes, please. The last thing we need is a trombonist lost in a dust storm with a truck full of music stands.

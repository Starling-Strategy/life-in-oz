Young Founders Circle Members' Survey Responses, September 2025

SYNTHETIC SOURCE — original fictional text; no real event or publication.
Format: survey_response
Theme: Young Founders

The following are selected responses from the Young Founders Circle's monthly member survey, conducted by Rowan Bell, Community Manager for the Emerald City Leadership Network. The theme for September was 'Groundwork'.

Question 1: What's a recent task—big or small—that felt foundational to your work?

Reuben Spindlecombe:
You know, it’s the invisible stuff, right? The big honey barrel was coming in fresh from the Quadling apiary first thing—our best local honey—and the dispenser had to be absolutely pristine. So I was there after closing, scrubbing it out with the long brush and boiling water. It’s not glamorous, but if you get that wrong, the whole batch tastes off. The smell of clean beeswax and honeycomb the next morning? That’s the payoff. It’s the foundation of the whole refill model: trust in what you’re putting in your bottle.

Ren Cinderholt:
The drive belt on the main millstone gave out near midnight, right after a late grain delivery. Couldn’t wait for the morning—peak harvest week, everyone counting on their flour. Had to get the spare belt from the loft, line up the pulleys, and get the tension just so. Took a few hours, and my hands were black with graphite grease, but the stones were turning by sunrise. Shutting down for a day would’ve backed up the whole Violet Hills supply chain. It’s not clever, it’s just necessary.

Question 2: What does 'community support' look like in your day-to-day?

Briony Gateholt:
It looks like thirty-odd blankets getting new corners and patched holes. We had a pile of donated ones at the warm room getting thin, and a bigger pile of fabric scraps. So we set an evening, put the kettle on, and anyone who could thread a needle showed up. It wasn’t about making them perfect, it was about making them warm again. Now they’re back in circulation. That’s the day-to-day: seeing a need you can meet with what’s already to hand.

Ozma Tipwell:
Right now, it looks like a stack of forty-three letters on my desk! We’re starting a pen-pal exchange with a civics class in the Quadling lowlands. My job is to coordinate the pairings, get the first batch addressed properly for the inter-country post, and make sure my students here in Westgate Mills understand the purpose is listening, not just reporting. It’s a logistical foundation—names, addresses, clear instructions—so a real connection can grow.

Question 3: What's a challenge you're currently puzzling through?

Reuben Spindlecombe:
Scale! But the good kind. How do I keep that hands-on, barrel-scrubbing care when we’re refilling twice as many containers? It’s a systems puzzle. Do I train someone specifically for the big equipment? Do I redesign the cleaning schedule? I don’t want to lose the direct touch, but I also need to sleep.

Ren Cinderholt:
Predictive maintenance. The belt snapped because it was old, not because of overload. I need a better logbook, a schedule to check wear before the harvest rush. It’s a dull puzzle, but a crucial one. A notebook and a calendar, that’s my current project.

Briony Gateholt:
Sustaining volunteer energy. The blanket evening was lovely, but it relied on the same five people who always show up. How do we make it a rotating duty, or tie it to a small perk, without turning a neighborly act into a transaction? That’s the puzzle for the next project.

Ozma Tipwell:
Managing expectations—the students’ and mine. This pen-pal project could fizzle if the first letters aren’t interesting or if replies are slow. I’m puzzling over how to frame it so the value is in the act of writing itself, not just the reply. We’ll see how the first mailing goes!

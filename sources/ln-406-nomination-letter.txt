A Nomination for Barnaby Stonefield, Meadow Mutual Aid

Theme: Meadow Mutual Aid

Lena Thistlecombe:
To the Founders’ Gala Committee,

I’m writing to nominate Barnaby Stonefield from Meadow Mutual Aid for a recognition award. I see his work from my end, as the keeper of the Bluebell Crossing community fridge. This nomination comes out of my own small lesson in order: a few months back, I had to clear out a whole shelf of food that had gone past its good date. It was heartbreaking. So I started a rule that every donation gets a clear date label right when it’s dropped off. It’s simple, but folks follow it now, and waste is way down.

That little system works because people like Barnaby get the food to us reliably. He’s the delivery rota lead, and his job is all about quiet, practical solutions when things go wrong. Back in the fall, our Thursday circuit was thrown into chaos. The usual shortcut through Reed Flats was shut for roadworks without any warning. For most of us, that just means a detour. For Barnaby, it meant an entire insulated delivery route was about to fail—if those boxes sat in the cart too long, the cooler packs would thaw and everything inside would spoil.

He didn’t just send a message saying there’d be delays. He replanned the whole Thursday circuit on the spot, figuring out a new sequence that added forty minutes of travel but kept every stop on time. He coordinated with the drivers, recalculated the ice-pack timelines, and made sure not a single delivery was missed or warmed. I know because our box arrived, cold as ever, right when it always does.

That’s Barnaby. No drama, just a slightly weary voice on the com-line working the problem until it’s solved. He makes sure the aid gets through, so the rest of us can do our part. He embodies what Meadow Mutual Aid is all about. Please consider him for this honor.

Sincerely,
Lena Thistlecombe
Bluebell Crossing Community Fridge

FOR: Emerald City Leadership Network – Small Grants Pool
RE: Grant funds disbursed for freelance audio documentation, August 2024
FROM: Yves Rushford

In July, I received a small grant from the network to support my independent sound work. I’d like to report on the use of those funds.

Yves Rushford:
The first project was a field recording commission. A composer, working on a piece about landscapes in the Gillikin and Quadling countries, needed very specific water textures. I used the grant to cover my travel to a secluded bend on a Quadling river, just south of the poppy fields. The goal was to capture the sound of water flowing over a particular type of smooth, mossy stone that’s common there—it has a softer, more layered quality than the sharper sounds you get from the rocky beds up near Munchkinland.

Yves Rushford:
I spent a full day there with my portable rig. It’s a quiet spot, no road noise, which is rare. The main challenge was wind; I had to wait for long lulls to get clean takes. I recorded from several positions: close-miking a single rivulet, a mid-distance capture of a small cascade, and a wide stereo ambience of the whole bend. I’ve delivered about forty-five minutes of edited, high-resolution audio to the composer. They were pleased, said it had exactly the resonant quality they were imagining.

The second initiative is a proposed community contribution, currently unfunded.

Yves Rushford:
Separately, I’ve been talking with the Festival Committee. They host a lot of readings and talks in the old City Assembly Hall, and the acoustics in there are… challenging. It’s all hard surfaces—stone, wood, glass. Great for a brass band, but for spoken word, it’s a reverberant mess. People in the back can’t understand the speaker.

Yves Rushford:
I’ve proposed to conduct a basic acoustic survey of the space. I’d use a calibrated microphone and speaker to measure the reverberation time—that’s how long sound hangs in the air—at different frequencies. Then, based on that data, I could recommend some affordable, non-permanent improvements. I’m thinking heavy curtains on a few walls, maybe some freestanding absorbent panels they could store when not in use. Nothing structural. I submitted the proposal to the committee last week; they’re reviewing it for their next planning cycle. It wouldn’t cost the network anything, but it felt relevant to mention as a direct outcome of the grant supporting my capacity to do this kind of work.

Yves Rushford:
The grant funds were used entirely for the Quadling trip: train fare, a hired cart for the last leg, and a small stipend for my day’s work. It allowed me to take on a project that was artistically meaningful but wouldn’t have covered my costs otherwise. Thank you for the support.

Yves Rushford

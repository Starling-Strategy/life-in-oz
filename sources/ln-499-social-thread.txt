Noor Brookmere:
Morning all. Had a good Saturday in the Meadowbank shop yard. Did a free patch clinic for inner tubes. We had twelve folks show up, from kids to grandparents. Spent an hour showing the steps: find the hole, scuff the area, apply the glue, wait, then press the patch on. Everyone got a little kit to take home. One parent said their kid’s bike has been out of commission for weeks over a simple puncture. Felt good to get them rolling again.

Isaac Holt:
Practical. I like that. Waste not. I’m working on something for the Timber Verge Repair Co-op volunteers. They’re always scrambling for tools, and their aprons are a mess of loops that let things fall out. I plan to adapt a set of leather aprons with proper sewn-in pockets. Each pocket will be sized for a specific tool—hammer, pry bar, tape measure. The mouth will angle so the tool sits snug. It’s in the planning stage; need to confirm final tool list with the crew lead.

Ambrose Bramblefield:
That sounds wonderfully organized, Isaac. I’ve had to adapt a bit myself this season. My usual supplier for Quadling summer berries had a complete crop failure. Rather than scrap the pie menu, I switched to using the preserved Gillikin cherries I put up last autumn, and paired them with the early rhubarb from the garden behind the cafe. The tartness works surprisingly well. The regulars haven’t complained; one said it tasted like 'a proper summer surprise.'

Beatrice Kettle:
Surplus! That’s my language. Had a local farm drop off two crates of tomatoes that were too soft to sell fresh. Beautiful flavor, just needed using. After closing up the canteen, I roasted the lot with some onions and herbs from the window box. Pureed it all down. Ended up with… let’s see… about thirty-two portions, by my count. Packed them in containers and froze them for the community kitchen. They’ll make a good base for a soup or a stew down the line.

A neighbor:
Beatrice, you’re a wonder. That sauce was used in a minestrone last week. My kids had two bowls each.

Noor Brookmere:
See? That’s it. It’s not always about the big sale. Sometimes it’s a patch kit, a pocket, a different berry, or a frozen portion. Keeps things moving.

Isaac Holt:
Precisely. A tool in the right pocket is a tool that gets used. A tomato that isn’t wasted is a resource. I’ll report back when I have a prototype apron.

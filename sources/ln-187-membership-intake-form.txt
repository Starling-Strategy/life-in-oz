Applicant Name: Hollis Wheatfield

Role/Organization: Librarian, Lantern Row Reading Room

Why do you want to join the Emerald City Leadership Network?

Hollis Wheatfield:
I believe in the connective tissue of shared spaces. The Reading Room is one such space, but it exists within a larger civic body. I hope to learn from the structured ways other circles manage resources, resolve scheduling conflicts, and preserve their own histories—the practical mechanics of community that aren't often written down in books.

Please describe a recent project or contribution you made to your community or workplace.

Hollis Wheatfield:
Well, there were two things last month, both rather quiet but necessary. First, I put together a small display in our front case on the city's original canal system. It's easy to forget we're built on managed water. I gathered maps from the Wizard's Office archives—beautiful, fading things—along with some engineers' sketches and photographs of the brickwork from the Munchkinland quarries. It's not a flashy display, but a few regulars have stopped to look, which is the point.

Hollis Wheatfield:
The other was more of a retrieval mission. After one of our story hours, a whole shelf on Oz architectural history was in disarray. Volumes on Gillikin timber frames were with the Quadling clay pamphlets, that sort of thing. I spent an afternoon tracking them down, all two dozen of them, and getting them back to their proper places. It’s a bit like detective work, following the cataloging logic. The room feels more settled when the order is restored.

What circle(s) are you most interested in connecting with?

Hollis Wheatfield:
The Reading Room Network, of course, to compare notes on circulation and preservation. And the Timber Verge Repair Co-op, perhaps. Their work with old structures must generate fascinating practical knowledge that complements our historical collections.

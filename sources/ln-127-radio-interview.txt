A Clear Record: Behind the Scenes at the Wizard's Office

SYNTHETIC SOURCE — original fictional text; no real event or publication.
Format: radio_interview
Theme: Wizard's Office Liaisons

Host:
Good afternoon and welcome back to ‘Emerald City Airwaves.’ I’m your host for this segment, Teo Orchardwood, founder of Orchardwood Translations and, as of this year, a liaison with the Wizard’s Office through our community network. Today we’re talking about what that liaison work looks like from the inside. My guest is Zora Oakholt, a records clerk at the Office itself. Welcome, Zora.

Zora Oakholt:
Thank you, Teo. It’s a pleasure to be here.

Host:
So, sometimes being a liaison is a formal title, and sometimes it’s just being the person who can help things move. I had a moment like that just last month over at the Green Market. A Gillikin grower—beautiful potatoes, by the way—was trying to finalize a bulk price with a grocer from the city center. The grower’s Gillikin dialect was a bit thick for the grocer’s city ears, and the negotiation was stalling on a phrase about ‘field-ripe’ versus ‘storage-ready.’

Zora Oakholt:
A critical distinction.

Host:
Exactly! So I stepped in, did a little real-time mediation—less translation, more clarifying intent—and they shook hands on a split delivery. One batch now, one batch after the first frost. It was small, but it got me thinking about your work. You’re dealing with documents that cross borders every day.

Zora Oakholt:
We are. My particular purview is the incoming paper. For years, trade license applications from the Winkie Country arrived, were stamped, and filed. The backlog was… considerable. A wall of gray cabinets. The problem was accessibility, predictability, and preservation.

Host:
Three excellent points.

Zora Oakholt:
Thank you. My solution was a quota. Not a dramatic push, but a steady one. Each clerk on my team now scans fifteen documents a day. No more, no less. It creates a rhythm. In three months, we’ve cleared two full cabinets. The files are now searchable by business name, by date, by trade type.

Host:
Fifteen a day. It sounds modest, but the cumulative effect—

Zora Oakholt:
Is a cleared shelf. It’s the difference between a planner saying ‘we’ll digitize the backlog’ and a clerk having a measurable, daily task. The work gets done. Now, when a Winkie metalworker needs to verify their license for a city contract, we can find it in moments, not hours.

Host:
That’s the connection, isn’t it? My market translation was about smoothing a single conversation. Your scanning quota is about building a system so that thousands of future conversations—between businesses and the Office—can start on solid ground. It’s all about removing the friction.

Zora Oakholt:
Precisely. Friction, delay, and uncertainty. Those are the three things we’re chipping away at, one document, one day at a time.

Host:
Zora Oakholt, thank you for giving us a look behind the curtain. For ‘Emerald City Airwaves,’ I’m Teo Orchardwood. We’ll be back after this short break.

Hazel said the milling songs transcription is complete. She worked from recordings a Gillikin elder made years ago. She noted the lyrical rhythms specifically matched the turning of the water wheels—when the verse would speed up or slow down, it tracked the work cycle. She compiled it into a small booklet and submitted it to the Reading Room archive last week.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Separately, she documented the history of the Heatherford primary school's bronze bell. She tracked down and interviewed three former students, now quite elderly, to record their memories. They described the specific, deep sound of the hand-cast bell marking the start of the school day, and how it carried across the fields. That documentation is also with the archive now.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Hazel mentioned she enjoyed both projects but found the bell history particularly moving, hearing how a sound connected people across decades. She's thinking of looking into other community sounds—market calls, ferry horns—but hasn't decided on a next project yet.

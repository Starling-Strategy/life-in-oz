Dear all,

I hope this finds you well, and that the late spring light is treating your reading nooks kindly. I’m writing to extend an invitation to the Lantern Row Reading Room for an evening I’ve been quietly arranging.

On the 25th of this month, we’ll be hosting a talk by a visiting scholar who has been studying the history of early printing presses in the Gillikin country. Their research touches on some fascinating, almost forgotten presses that operated near the Perilous Mountains, using local paper stocks and typefaces cast from Gillikin alloys. It’s not just about the machinery, of course, but about what those presses allowed to circulate—broadside ballads, guild ordinances, philosophical tracts. The way ink settles on a page has its own story.

The talk will begin at 7 PM. I’ve cleared the long table by the west window to give us space. I do hope some of you can join us.

Warmly,
Hollis

Hi Hollis, and everyone,

That sounds like a wonderful evening. The connection between regional materials and what gets published is so important. I’ll certainly try to be there.

It reminds me of something we just did here at the Junction Quarter school. Our middle-grade classes are in a unit on Oz civic design—looking at why our public squares are shaped the way they are, how the Brickway routes were planned, that sort of thing. To complement it, I arranged a video call last week with a historian from the Emerald City archives. She shared original sketches of early fountain designs and spoke about the debates over using local stone versus imported brick for civic buildings. The students were riveted, especially by a drawing of a proposed (but never built) triple-tiered clock tower for Munchkinland. It really brought the concepts off the page for them.

Maybe there’s a way to connect these threads for older students someday. In any case, thank you for organizing, Hollis.

Best,
Aurelia

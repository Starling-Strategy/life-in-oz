Gus Crowby:
Gus Crowby here. Westgate Mills. Had a call last week from the old grain mill out by the verge. Their conveyor’s main drive gear was so worn it was skipping teeth. No spare to be had, of course. Brought the mangled thing back to the shop, spent two days matching the tooth profile on the lathe. Got her cut, hardened, and back in place yesterday. System’s humming now. Just a gear, but it keeps the flour flowing.

Hattie Tinstead:
That’s wonderful work, Gus. It’s all connected, isn’t it? We had a similar story here on Tinsmith Row. The super at the old apartment block on Cobble Lane was swamped, so I pulled together a dedicated plumbing kit from the tool library—pipe wrench, basin wrench, seat wrench, the works. Left it with the residents. They’ve already used it to tackle three minor leaks in the common areas themselves. Saved a call-out fee and learned a bit in the process.

Dov Quadford:
Dov Quadford. Good. South Copse community hall’s stage wiring was a fire waiting to happen. Frayed cables everywhere, no proper control. The co-op had a new dimmer switch in the donation pile. I rewired the whole rig last weekend: new cables, proper conduit, installed the dimmer. Lights work smoothly now. Safer. Lets them put on their little plays without worrying about sparks.

Ursa Saffronridge:
Ursa Saffronridge from Lantern Row. Hearing about these hands-on fixes is so inspiring. My contribution is still in the planning stages, I’m afraid. I’m still gathering materials for the display frames—looking for some reclaimed molding. It’s a slower project, but I hope it’ll spark some conversations.

Gus Crowby:

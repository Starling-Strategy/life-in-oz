A haulier, a union steward, and a foreman walk into a social board...

SYNTHETIC SOURCE — original fictional text; no real event or publication.
Format: social_thread
Theme: Brickway Transit Union

Abel Wickridge:
Posted to The Yellow Brick Board: Sharing loads. My rigs were running empty half the week just coming back from the city. Waste of fuel, wear, and my time. Reached out to another haulier out of Westgate. We’re swapping manifests now. If my truck’s dropping in the Emerald City and his is headed out empty, he takes my return load. We split the margin. Simple. Cuts my deadhead runs by more than half. Anyone else doing something similar on the eastern Brickway routes?

A dispatcher:
Abel, that’s smart. We’ve talked about a load board for years. Is it just a handshake deal?

Abel Wickridge:
Handshake and a ledger. We’re both old enough to know a good deal when we see one. No board needed, just a phone call.

Kerensa Hedgecombe:
Direct trade works. My crew just cleared a jungle of an old lot for the quarry owner down past Yellow Kiln. Two days of brush and sapling work. In return, we’re getting a full load of grade-A gravel for the culvert job on the West Mill road, at a fraction of the cost. Keeps the project budget intact and the quarry owner’s land usable again. Sometimes you just have to talk to the person with the pile of rocks.

Sable Gateby:
That’s the kind of practical mutual aid that keeps projects moving. Speaking of practical support, I’ve been hearing from members that the push for a waterproof gear stipend is getting somewhere. The word from the last bargaining session is that management has agreed in principle to a small annual allowance for outdoor crews. It’s not signed yet—details on the amount and eligibility are still being finalized—but it’s on the table. It’s a start.

A road crew member:
Sable, that’s huge. My boots haven’t been truly dry since last fall. Any idea when we might see details?

Sable Gateby:
Not yet. The committee meets again next week. I’ll post an update on the union board when we have something concrete. Don’t go spending the imaginary money just yet.

Abel Wickridge:
Good. A dry worker is a safe worker. And a load in the truck is a profitable truck. Different scales, same idea.

Meadow Mutual Aid Network Check-In: November 2025

SYNTHETIC SOURCE — original fictional text; no real event or publication.
Format: post_event_debrief
Theme: Meadow Mutual Aid

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Post-event debrief: Meadow Mutual Aid Network Check-In, November 2025.

Attended: Ezra Hedgebourne (Blue Orchard), Sable Orchardfield (Eastfield), Odile Pepperby (Lantern Row), Calla Copperby (Bluebell Crossing), Briony Oakmere (Gate Quarter). Rowan Bell present as facilitator.

General atmosphere was energetic and positive. A lot of cross-pollination of ideas between different regions and types of work (care, transport, food, goods). Focus was on sharing what's been done lately and what's being looked at, no formal decisions required.

Item 1: Updates from the field.
- Ezra reported on the new mending sessions at the Blue Orchard community center. He connected a retired seamstress who wanted to help with the network. They've set up a weekly two-hour slot. In the first month, they repaired about twenty items—clothes, torn curtains, a backpack. It's become a social spot; a few people stay just to chat even if they don't have mending. Ezra emphasized the 'we' of it: 'We're keeping skills in the community and saving people a trip to the tailor.'
- Odile gave a quiet, dry update from Lantern Row. She worked directly with the pharmacy on Brickway that serves the care home. They used to deliver prescriptions on three different days depending on the supplier, which was confusing for staff and sometimes led to delays. Odile got them to consolidate to one dedicated delivery day. 'Now the boxes arrive, I sign for them, and the residents get their medications on time. It's not glamorous, but it prevents a dozen small crises a month.'
- Calla was very direct about the produce run. She set up an arrangement with the Quadling farmers market to collect unsold but still fresh produce at closing time. The first run was last week. Meadow Mutual Aid volunteers with carts met the vendors, loaded up, and distributed the food to forty families in Bluebell Crossing and the southern Emerald City precincts that same evening. 'No storage, no fuss. Food moved from where it was surplus to where it was needed in about three hours.'
- Briony described the coat drive initiative. She got permission to place a clearly marked bin at the front of her tram on the Gate Quarter-to-Market Square route. For one week, she made announcements and kept an eye on it. She collected over thirty gently used coats. 'People would get on, see the bin, and sometimes get off at the next stop to go home and fetch a coat. It was very simple.' The coats have been passed to the Mutual Aid network for sorting and distribution.

Item 2: Ideas in progress.
- Sable mentioned that other members of Meadow Mutual Aid have been talking to her about an idea. She's heard from several parents in the network that there's interest in a toy and book lending library. The Eastfield Childcare Co-op has a storage shed that isn't fully used. Sable is looking into the feasibility—what it would take to clean it out, set up shelves, and create a simple check-out system. She was clear: 'We're just at the looking-into-it stage. I'm hearing the need, so we're seeing if the co-op space can be part of the solution.'

Item 3: Open discussion and cross-connections.
- Ezra asked Briony if the tram collection could be a recurring thing, maybe for blankets in the deep winter. Briony said she'd ask her supervisor but thinks a weekend drive might be easier to manage than a full week.
- Odile asked Calla if the produce distribution included areas near care homes, suggesting some residents on fixed incomes might benefit. Calla said the current route doesn't but they can adjust; she'll get the route list to Odile to see if any stops align.
- Sable's library idea got a lot of positive murmurs. Ezra offered to connect her with the retired seamstress, who he said has a lot of children's books she's been meaning to find a home for.

Item 4: Next steps and actions.
- Calla will share the produce distribution route with Odile by end of week. (Action: Calla)
- Briony will inquire about a weekend blanket drive on the trams and report back. (Action: Briony)
- Sable will draft a simple proposal for the shed library to share with the co-op board and the wider network for feedback. (Action: Sable)
- Rowan to circulate these notes and check in on actions in December.

No major logistical hurdles identified. The group agreed to check in again in the new year, possibly with a focus on winter preparedness projects.

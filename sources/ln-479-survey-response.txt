Emerald City Leadership Network – Members' Survey, June 2026

1. What's a recent project or adjustment in your work you're particularly satisfied with?

Lux Thistlefield:
I channeled my inner dramatist for a student struggling with regional waterway history. The Munchkin canal system's founding and expansion is a saga of mud, sweat, and engineering ingenuity, but the textbook makes it drier than a brick in the sun. So I created a set of illustrated flashcards—my own hand-drawn lock gates, pickaxe-wielding Munchkins, the whole glorious mess. The student aced the regional exam, and more importantly, she told me she finally 'saw the story.' That's the sweet spot.

2. What's a challenge you're actively working on or preparing for right now?

Sunniva Fernmere:
Flavor alchemy. The autumn menu needs a star savory tart. I'm riding the line between hearty and bright. Roasted squash for that deep, sweet base, spiced lentils for protein and warmth, maybe a flash of apple cider vinegar to cut through. Textures are key—the squash has to be creamy, not mushy. I'm doing a trial run next week at my usual Junction Quarter spot. Samples for the regulars. They'll tell me straight if it's a hit or needs to go back in the oven, metaphorically speaking.

3. Give an example of solving a concrete, physical problem for a client or your space recently.

Imogen Shaw:
Junction Quarter library. Their main meeting room's wireless kept dropping during video calls. Frustrating. Went onsite, found the access point was tucked behind a metal cabinet—a basic signal killer. Repositioned it. Then traced the cable run and found it was routed right alongside a cluster of old water pipes. That'll cause interference all day. Added some simple shielding to the cable, rerouted it a few inches away. Connection's been solid since. Sometimes it's not the tech, it's the placement.

4. How are you adapting your service or operations to meet a specific local demand?

Selwyn Larkworth:
Speed and temperature. Got a new contract with that fantastic Gate Quarter bakery. They need their fresh bagels at three different coffee shops by 7 AM, still warm. That's a tight window. Our cargo bikes are perfect for the narrow Quarter lanes in the early morning, but we had to nail the heat retention. Designed new insulated waxed-canvas panniers—sturdy, waterproof, and they keep the heat in. Did a test run last week; delivered at 6:50 AM and the bagels were still steaming. The coffee shops are happy, the bakery's happy. Efficient delivery that doesn't sacrifice quality.

5. What's one piece of advice you'd give to someone just starting out in your field?

Lux Thistlefield:
Find the narrative. Whether it's quadratic equations or canal locks, there's a human story underneath. Your job is to be the translator, turning abstract concepts into something your student can connect with. Enthusiasm is contagious; if you're bored, they're lost.

Sunniva Fernmere:
Taste everything, trust your regulars. Your own palate is your first guide, but the people who come back every week? They're your real critics. Listen to them. And always have a backup plan for when the market runs out of your main ingredient.

Imogen Shaw:
Look at the physical setup first. People will blame the software or the service provider, but half the time it's a cable, a power strip, or something blocking a signal. Walk the space. It saves hours of remote diagnostics.

Selwyn Larkworth:
Solve a real, tangible problem for your neighbors. Don't just have a cool idea. Is there a delivery gap? A traffic snarl? A need for speed? Build your service around that specific pain point. And invest in good gear from the start; it pays off in reliability.

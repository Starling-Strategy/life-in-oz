October 2024
Emerald City, Oz – The Wizard's Office Liaisons circle of the Emerald City Leadership Network bridges the gap between civic process and practical skill. Two recent events, one in the Westgate Foundry and another at the Winkie Works Guild hall, highlight how members apply their professional expertise to strengthen community projects.

Last month, community grants officer Roland Spindlemere reviewed an application for a shared bread oven in a small Gillikin neighborhood. The proposal was complete save for one detail: the cost estimates for the firebox's clay bricks listed a material type but lacked a formal quote from a supplier or mason. "Without that line item, the whole budget section was technically incomplete," Spindlemere explained. "I had to pause the review and ask the group to get a written estimate. Hardly headline news, but getting it right upfront prevents delays later when the funds are actually disbursed." The group obtained the quote, and the application is now back on track for a final decision.

Roland Spindlemere:
It’s not about creating obstacles. It’s about making sure the foundation is solid before we build. A missing quote might seem minor, but it can hold up orders, contractors, and the whole project timeline. My job is to help groups see those gaps before they become problems.

Meanwhile, lathe operator Jory Saffronwood recently spent an evening at the Winkie Works Guild hall demonstrating a fundamental metalworking technique. Using a piece of scrap aluminum rod, Saffronwood showed a dozen attendees, including several apprentices, how to properly set up a lathe to cut precise screw threads. The hands-on session focused on tool alignment, speed settings, and measuring pitch.

Jory Saffronwood:
Threading is one of those skills that looks simple until you try it. If your setup is off by a hair, the thread binds or strips. We walked through each step slowly—checking the compound rest angle, selecting the right cutting tool, making the initial pass. The goal was to give people the confidence to try it themselves on their shop’s equipment, using scrap material first.

The Liaisons circle connects members from the Wizard's Office with tradespeople and makers across Oz. By sharing their distinct forms of knowledge—whether it's navigating grant requirements or the precise setup of a machine—they help ensure community initiatives are both well-supported and well-built.

About the Emerald City Leadership Network: The Network is an association of working adults across Oz’s four countries and the Emerald City, organized into circles based on profession and civic interest. The Wizard's Office Liaisons circle facilitates direct communication and shared learning between civic administration and the wider community.

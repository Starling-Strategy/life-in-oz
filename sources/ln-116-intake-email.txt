Beryl Cloverton:
Notes from conversation with Nell Heatherholt, Violet Hills tool library steward, early November.

Regarding my own situation: My repair van is a disaster. A couple of wrenches live under the passenger seat, and I once found a socket rolling around near the brake pedal. Not ideal. I intend to install some modular shelving units along the interior walls—the kind you can bolt in and move the brackets later. This should let me organize tools by job type and, more importantly, safely carry more compact appliances per trip. I won't have a blender trying to make friends with a soldering iron while I'm driving anymore. I'm sourcing the brackets from the old millworks in Yellow Kiln; they have scrap angle iron that'll do the job fine. It's a winter project for when call-outs slow down.

Nell's project:
Nell is compiling a simple, illustrated booklet on basic tool safety and care. She says she's aiming it at teenagers using the library for the first time, who might be helping with a home fix or starting a school project. The booklet will cover things like how to carry a saw safely, why you clean a drill bit, and what 'righty-tighty, lefty-loosey' actually means. She's keeping the text short and plans to use clear line drawings done by a friend who tutors at the library. She's gathering anecdotes from regulars about common first-time mistakes to make it relatable. The goal is to have a draft by the end of the year to share with other lending libraries in the network.

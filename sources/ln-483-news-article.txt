Emerald City Network Notes: The Parade's Path, a Bakery's Panels, and a Golden Thread

For the Emerald City Leadership Network's Festival Committee, the work of celebration happens in the details—the routes, the power, the stitches, and the bandages. Four members recently shared updates on their ongoing projects, from a triumphant parade to the practical preparations for the next one.

Keeping the Band on the Brickway

The Spectacle Lane Brass Band’s performance at the Emerald City Spring Parade was a clear, marching success, largely thanks to Linus Barleyfield’s logistical orchestration. Ahead of the event, Barleyfield meticulously charted the band’s route, identified three rest stops along the Brickway, and secured a quiet side-street for a final warm-up.

Linus Barleyfield:
You can’t just tootle onto the avenue and hope for the best. It’s a piece of music, see? The route is the score, the stops are the rests, and if you don’t plan the transitions, the whole thing falls flat. We hit our first note right on the downbeat and played coherently all the way to the palace gates.

Band members confirmed the smooth execution, arriving on time and finishing the parade with energy to spare.

A Bakery’s Brighter Bottom Line

In Blue Orchard, the hum of a new solar array is now part of the morning bakery chorus. Basil Coppershaw of Coppershaw Solar oversaw the installation of panels on the roof of a local bread and pie shop this past spring.

Basil Coppershaw:
The owner’s main load is the oven—it’s a constant, predictable draw. Perfect for solar offset. We sized the array to cover the bulk of its daytime operation. It’s a clean, direct conversion: sunlight to kilowatts to crusty loaves.

The bakery owner reported a noticeable drop in the electricity bill over the last quarter, crediting the panels for keeping costs down during the busiest baking hours.

The Golden Thread That Got Away

Not every preparation goes perfectly. Wynn Fernshaw, who crafted the intricate banner that led the Quadling contingent in the parade, hit a snag in her Blue Orchard workshop. Midway through embroidering a sunburst motif, she ran out of a specific spool of gold thread.

Wynn Fernshaw:
It was that particular bright, warm gold I’d used for the central rays. My supplier was out, and the parade was days away. I had to switch to a spool I had on hand—a shade cooler, with a touch more silver in it. To the casual eye, it’s still gold. But I see the join. It’s a quiet lesson: always check your spools before you start a sun.

The banner was completed and admired, its slight variation in hue known only to its maker.

Planning for the Next Bump or Bruise

Looking ahead to future festivals and daily community needs, Mabry Brickford of Quadling Health Access is focusing on readiness. She has proposed a systematic review of the communal first aid kits stored in Redfern Hill’s public buildings—the community hall, the reading room, and the market shelter.

Mabry Brickford:
After the Spring Parade, we used up the bandages from the kit at the hall. It’s a good sign people knew where to find them, but it means the kit needs restocking before the next event. I’m suggesting a simple quarterly stocktake. Someone checks each kit, notes what’s low or expired, and we top them up. It’s not glamorous, but it means we’re ready.

The proposal is currently being discussed with the Redfern Hill community council, with Brickford hoping to implement the plan by the end of the summer.

From the macro-logistics of a marching band to the micro-detail of a single thread, these efforts highlight the Festival Committee’s broad definition of its work: ensuring the show goes on, sustainably and safely, from the first note to the last bandage.

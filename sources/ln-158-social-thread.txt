Pim Plumstead:

Oriel Emerystead:
Oh, that's a lovely percussive accent to the whole operation, Pim! Coordination like that sets the rhythm. Speaking of stages—literally—we had a quiet prelude before our summer series. The old bandstand in Hammerhead Rise park had wiring that was... let's call it a dissonant chord waiting to resolve. Ansel Wickby:
Good work on the bandstand. Solid. Heard something similar about the Lantern Festival. A popcorn machine behind a food stall went down. Vendor was facing a long, quiet night. Story I got is that someone fixed it using parts from a broken hot plate that was just sitting there. Cannibalized the right pieces, got the machine humming again. Saved the stall.

A food vendor:
That was me! The popcorn machine’s main heating coil gave out. I was ready to pack it in. This repairer—didn’t catch his name—just looked at it, then at the old hot plate I use for backup, and ten minutes later he’s swapped parts. Machine ran perfectly the rest of the night. Whoever that was, thank you.

Ansel Wickby:
Sounds like a clean diagnostic. Identify the faulty component, find a donor with a compatible one. No need for a new machine, just a little resourcefulness.

Oriel Emerystead:
A beautiful improvisation! Taking the melody from one instrument to save another. That’s the spirit.

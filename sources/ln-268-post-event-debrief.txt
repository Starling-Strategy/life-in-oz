Rowan Bell · Community Manager · Timber Verge Repair Co-op debrief · June 2025

Post-meetup notes. Held at the South Meadow community hall. About fifteen attendees, a mix of co-op members and folks from the network's other circles who have an interest in woodwork, tools, or logistics.

Arthur Keene was there, of course, holding court by the tea urn. He didn't mention the dining table himself, but Hattie Tinstead brought it up. She said she’d heard from a neighbor in Quadling Country that Arthur had taken on a big old dining table missing its extension supports. The owner thought it was junk. Arthur apparently spent three days picking through scrap oak at the back of the Timber Verge yard, found pieces with the right grain, and hand-cut new supports so the leaf fits snug and doesn’t wobble. The neighbor is thrilled; says the table seats the whole family again. Arthur just nodded slowly and said, 'It’s all in the joint. A sliding dovetail, if you take your time, will outlast the tree it came from.' Then he launched into a story about a Gillikin cedar chest from sixty years ago.

Hattie Tinstead showed up with a canvas roll. She laid out a half-dozen pairs of fabric shears on the table. They were the ones from the Tinsmith Row community centre’s sewing group. She said last month the group was ready to throw them out, they were so dull they’d just chew fabric. She took them all, used a honing guide to get the angle right every time, and worked them on an oilstone. She demonstrated on one pair, cutting through a scrap of thick wool felt like it was butter. The shears are back in circulation. She mentioned she’s going to run a sharpening workshop at the tool library next quarter if there’s interest.

Talk of the tool library brought up Teo Brickholt’s name. A couple of people from the Brickway Transit Union said they’d heard Teo’s been instrumental in getting a neighborhood tool library started over in Green Market. He’s not just donating surplus hand tools—screwdrivers, wrenches, a couple of hand planes—from his hardware store, but he’s also been advising on the check-out system. He suggested a simple ledger and tags, something physical that doesn’t rely on everyone having a scroll-screen. They’re still in the planning stages, but they have a shelf unit and a donated lockbox now.

Dacey Barleybourne was in from Gillikin Falls. Gruff as ever, but had a good story. Said a delivery of rye came into the mill with higher moisture than usual. Could have sat and sweated, gone moldy in the silo. Instead of just processing it on the usual schedule, Dacey diverted the batch. Had the crew spread it all in the shallow storage bins overnight, cranked up the air circulation. Let it sit for a full day. They processed it the next morning, and the yield was fine. No must, no waste. Dacey said it was just paying attention. 'Grain tells you what it needs, if you listen.'

Abel Wickridge was the last to share. He drives freight out of Eastfield. Said there was a major snarl on the main Eastfield approach last Tuesday—a wagon lost a wheel, blocked both lanes. Dispatch was telling everyone to wait. Abel knew the schedule was tight for a load of milled timber headed to a framing crew in Emerald City. He remembered an old quarry road that cuts behind the hill. It’s rough, seldom-used, but passable if you take it slow. He took the detour, bumped along for forty minutes, and rejoined the Brickway past the blockage. Delivered the pallets only twenty minutes late. The foreman at the site didn’t even know there’d been a problem. Abel shrugged. 'Better a dusty truck than a missed deadline.'

Open actions from the meetup: Hattie will draft a date for the sharpening workshop and send it to the network calendar. A few folks asked if the tool library system Teo’s helping with could be a template for other neighborhoods; I’ll reach out to Teo to see if he’s willing to share notes. Arthur mentioned the co-op’s scrap pile is getting a bit too 'curated'—needs a proper sort-through. Dacey and Abel got talking about grain shipments and road conditions; might be a useful connection for the network’s logistics circle to follow up on.

Overall mood was focused and practical. Less about big ideas, more about the specific, tangible fixes people are doing in their own patches. Good energy.

FOR THE ATTENTION OF THE EMERALD CITY SMALL BUSINESS DEVELOPMENT FUND

REPORT ON GRANT #ECSBDF-24-09-YF
GRANT AMOUNT: 400 EMERALDS
REPORTING PERIOD: JULY–SEPTEMBER 2024
SUBMITTED BY: THE EMERALD CITY LEADERSHIP NETWORK, YOUNG FOUNDERS CIRCLE LIAISON

This report details the use of funds from Grant #ECSBDF-24-09-YF, awarded to support collaborative skill-sharing and resource development within the Young Founders Circle. The grant was divided into two equal allocations of 200 Emeralds each, designated for materials and refreshments for member-led workshops.

Allocation 1 (200 Emeralds): Materials for Collaborative Workshop Kits
This allocation was used by Ida Poppyford of Poppyford Design Studio to create a standardized, reusable supply kit for our craft and design workshops. Ida purchased durable storage containers, compartment dividers, and labeling materials. The core of the project was her design and production of a set of clear, icon-based labels. These labels, which feature simple pictorial representations of items like bone folders, linen thread, and cutting mats, were affixed to each compartment. The system makes it effortless for participants—especially those new to the crafts—to identify, use, and, crucially, return materials to their proper places. This has already reduced setup and cleanup time for our hosted workshops by nearly half, according to post-workshop feedback.

Ida Poppyford:
The whole point was to remove the friction of 'where does this go?' so people can focus on learning the skill, not hunting for a slot. Seeing someone immediately put a awl back in the right spot because they recognized the little picture? That’s the win.

Allocation 2 (200 Emeralds): Workshop Refreshments
This allocation was managed by Lux Thistlefield of Thistlefield Tutoring to provide sustenance during extended skill-share sessions. Funds were used to purchase bread, cheese, butter, fruit, and tea. Lux has a reported talent for turning these into simple, hearty snacks. Parents of students who attend her tutoring sessions have mentioned that she often prepares toasted cheese sandwiches during late-night exam prep. She brought this same energy to our Young Founders workshops, ensuring that participants working through complex topics or hands-on projects had access to warm, filling food without leaving the workspace.

Lux Thistlefield:
Brain fuel! You can’t ask people to grapple with a new business model canvas or a tricky stitch on an empty stomach. A bit of melted cheese does wonders for morale and concentration. It doesn’t sound like much, but it says ‘we care you’re here, and we want you to stay focused.’

Unfinished Work & Next Steps
The initial supply kits, funded by this grant, are now in active circulation. However, demand for workshops has increased, indicating a need for at least one additional kit to serve simultaneous or larger sessions. This represents an unfunded need. The refreshment model has been successfully established as a best practice for our longer meetings, and future grant requests may seek to formalize this into a modest recurring budget line. The Young Founders Circle extends its sincere thanks to the Emerald City Small Business Development Fund for this supportive investment in our collaborative infrastructure.

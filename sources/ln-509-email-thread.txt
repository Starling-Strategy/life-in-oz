Rowan,

Following up on our last meeting. I’ve been looking at the waste stream from our glazing and trimming stations. The amount of reclaimable clay slip and trimmings that still goes into general waste is a bit higher than I’d like. I’m suggesting we install a dedicated, sealed recycling bin for that specific material in the main studio area. It’s a small change, but if we get the sorting right from the start, I think we could see material waste drop by about ten percent. It would just need a clear label and a quick briefing for everyone using the space.

Regards,
Rufus

Rufus, Rowan,

Rufus’s proposal sounds like a sensible step.

Separately, I’ve been working on something that came out of our conversation about tool maintenance for smaller shops. I’m drafting a proposal to the Wizard’s Office Liaisons. The idea is a guild-run sharpening service. We’d set up a mobile station—or perhaps a few fixed locations—to handle lathe tools, chisels, and plane irons for the small metalworking shops in the Winkie and Quadling countries. Many of them don’t have the equipment or time to keep edges properly honed, and it affects their work. The draft is still in progress; I’m working out the schedule and cost recovery model.

Best,
Jory

All,

Rufus, the bin idea is good. Jory, let me know if you want a hand with the tool service specs.

On my end: I got tired of guessing when tools needed a touch-up. Created a simple tag system for the lathe stations here at Westgate. Three colors: green for sharp, yellow for nearing due, red for needs work now. Each tool gets a dated tag when it’s serviced. It’s cut down on the chatter and poor finish jobs we were seeing. The downtime between changes is shorter now, but more predictable. The finish quality is noticeably better. That’s it.

Juniper

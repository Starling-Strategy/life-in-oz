Quiet Fixes & Bright Ideas: Festival Committee Members Shine in Everyday Roles

SYNTHETIC SOURCE — original fictional text; no real event or publication.
Format: newsletter_feature
Theme: Festival Committee

While the Emerald City Leadership Network's Festival Committee is best known for its vibrant public celebrations, its members' talents for coordination and creating the right atmosphere extend far beyond parade routes and stages. This month, we’re highlighting two Festival Committee members whose recent, quiet projects in their home communities have made a world of difference.

In Meadowbank, Percy Pepperwick of the Meadowbank Tenants’ Association looked at the dimly lit hallways of his four-story building and saw an opportunity for a simple upgrade. 'We were all buying the same old bulbs separately,' he said. 'It was inefficient in every way.' Percy coordinated a collective effort, knocking on doors to collect contributions for a bulk purchase of modern, energy-efficient bulbs. He then recruited a volunteer from each floor to handle the installations on their own landing. The result? Well-lit common areas and a small but noticeable drop in the building's shared electricity costs.

Percy Pepperwick:
It wasn’t about a grand gesture. It was about taking a shared problem and making the solution accessible to everyone. When people see a well-lit hallway, they feel a bit more invested in the place. That’s the foundation.

Over in Westgate Mills, Phoebe Thistleholt of the Westgate Mills Childcare Co-op tackled a different kind of ambiance challenge: nap time. The previous system relied on complete silence, which often had the paradoxical effect of making every tiny rustle or sigh feel magnified. Phoebe compiled a gentle, continuous playlist of recorded outdoor sounds—distant wind chimes, a light rain, a soft breeze through leaves. The change was immediate.

Phoebe Thistleholt:
The complete quiet was almost tense. With the new sounds, there’s a gentle blanket of noise that actually helps the room settle. The children drift off more quickly, and the caregivers can move around without feeling like we’re going to disturb the whole room. It’s made the afternoons smoother for everyone.

Whether it’s the practical glow of a new lightbulb or the soothing backdrop of a simulated shower, these projects remind us that building a better environment often starts with attentive, shared solutions. The Festival Committee is proud to have such thoughtful organizers in its ranks, people who know that the right atmosphere—be it for a festival or a nap—is always a collective creation.

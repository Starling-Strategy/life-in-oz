FOR REPORTING TO THE EMERALD CITY LEADERSHIP NETWORK MICRO-GRANT FUND
Submitted: September 2024
Project Lead: Small Business Circle

This report summarizes the use of the $1,500 micro-grant awarded in May 2024 to support small business community-building initiatives in Yellow Kiln, Winkie Country, and Blue Orchard, Munchkin Country. Funds were allocated for materials and small incentives to encourage local collaboration.

In Yellow Kiln, Aurelia Quadwood of Quadwood's Barbers used the grant to print and distribute a cross-promotional coupon booklet. The booklet features discounts from her barbershop, the Yellow Kiln Bakery, and the Kilnside Hardware Store. The goal was to encourage customers to shop across town rather than traveling to larger markets. She printed 500 booklets, which were placed at each of the three participating businesses.

Aurelia Quadwood:
It was a simple idea, really. We all know each other, but our customers might not think to wander down the street. The coupons give them a little nudge. I used the grant for the nice, sturdy paper and the printing—something folks would keep in their pocket, not just toss away. The bakery’s been handing them out with every loaf, and I’ve seen a few new faces come in for a trim who said they got their coupon at the hardware store.

The remaining funds supported a materials purchase for Golda Hedgestead of Hedgestead Upholstery in Blue Orchard. The local community hall’s chairs were in poor condition, with worn and torn fabric. A neighbor had donated several large bolts of durable cloth but lacked the fasteners, thread, and specialized tools needed for the job.

Golda Hedgestead:
The fabric donation was a blessing, but it was just the start. The grant let me buy the industrial-strength thread, the proper webbing, and a set of new needles for my machine. Without that, I’d have been patching, not properly restoring. I’ve finished sixty chairs for the hall. They’re not just covered; they’re rebuilt to last. It’s quiet work, but you can see the difference—the whole room feels more cared for now.

Funds have been fully expended. The $1,500 was used as follows: $320 for printing and paper for the Yellow Kiln coupon booklets, $1,180 for upholstery supplies (thread, webbing, needles, and fasteners) for the Blue Orchard community hall project. Both initiatives are complete. The coupon booklets continue to circulate, and the community hall chairs are in regular use. No further funding is requested for these completed projects.

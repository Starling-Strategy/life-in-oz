Emerald City Leadership Network – Members' Survey – March 2025

Question 1: What's a project you're currently working on or planning that you're excited about?

Quill Orchardley:
Oh, it's the catalog. Our seed library in the public library is really coming along – jars and packets on the shelves, a good bit of traffic. But folks have to browse the jars to see what we've got, and sometimes the handwriting on the labels can be a bit, well, artistic. I'm planning to sit down and type up a proper catalog. Not just a list, mind you. I want to include planting notes for each variety: depth, spacing, days to germination, whether it prefers a little shade or full sun. The sort of thing a new gardener can take home and feel confident with. It'll live at the circulation desk, and maybe we can post a copy on the community board too.

Question 2: Share a recent small win or a piece of knowledge you gained that felt significant.

Quill Orchardley:
Just last week, one of our members brought in a dusty old jar of beans from her attic. No label, just these lovely mottled purple and white beans. She said her grandmother had saved them. I spent an evening with my own garden journals – I've kept sketches of bean pods and seeds for years – and I'm almost certain they're an old Gillikin Runner variety. The pod shape in my sketch matched her memory of them, and the seed pattern is distinctive. It's not a commercial variety anymore. It felt like a little piece of history, sitting there in my kitchen. We've got them properly labeled now, and we'll grow a few out this season to see if we can revive the line.

Question 3: What kind of support from the wider network would be most useful for your circle right now?

Quill Orchardley:
Honestly, just knowing other circles are out there doing their own versions of this is support enough. Maybe if anyone in the Growers circle has experience putting together a simple printed guide, I'd love to hear what format worked for them. But mostly, it's just nice to be part of the conversation.

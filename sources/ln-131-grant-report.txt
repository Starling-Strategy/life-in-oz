FOR RELEASE
December 2024

This report details the use of funds provided by the Emerald City Leadership Network Coordination Grant, disbursed in June 2024, to the Brickway Transit Union. The grant supported member-led initiatives addressing workplace conditions and community service connections within the Union's scope.

The primary project supported was the formalization of a member feedback and action system for depot facilities. Grant funds covered the time for Union Steward Albin Gatemere to compile, document, and persistently follow up on member-submitted maintenance requests. The most notable outcome was the resolution of a long-standing issue at the Brickway depot.

Albin Gatemere:
The ceiling fan in the main break room had been rattling like a wagon on cobbles for months. Drivers were getting headaches. I collected written notes from three separate drivers detailing the noise and the lack of airflow, submitted it as a single, documented request, and followed up every other day. The new unit they installed is so much quieter you can actually hear yourself think in there now. It took a week from submission to completion.

The grant also facilitated improved communication channels between different roles sharing the Brickway Junction depot. While not a formal Union initiative, this cross-talk is a direct benefit of the network's emphasis on connection.

Quentin Birch:
It wasn't anything official. A courier mentioned to me that the depot manager was thinking of shifting the afternoon collection time later. I know a few of the shop fronts on my route rely on that timing, so I passed the feedback along. Just made sure the right person heard it.

A portion of the grant was allocated to recognizing and supporting the community service ethos already present among Union members. This is not a paid program, but the network funding allowed for the creation of a simple acknowledgment process, encouraging members to share these stories as examples of the Union's role in the city.

Joss Clovercombe:
It was just the decent thing to do. She was getting off at my stop on Spectacle Lane, the steep part, with two bags of groceries heavier than they looked. I finished my safety check, carried them up to her doorstep, and made sure she was inside. It meant pausing my schedule for a few minutes, but the route had a bit of buffer. Anyone would have done it.

Funds were spent on a small stipend for the Union steward's coordination hours (approx. 15 hours total over six months) and administrative costs for documentation. The total grant of 50 silver crowns has been fully expended.

Unfinished work includes the ongoing need to systematize the feedback loop for depot cross-tenant issues and to explore a more structured, voluntary driver assist protocol for vulnerable passengers in hilly zones like Spectacle Lane. These are topics for future network discussion, not currently funded projects.

About the Brickway Transit Union: The Union represents drivers, maintenance workers, and depot staff along the Emerald City's primary transit corridor. It advocates for safe working conditions, reliable public transit, and the role of transit workers in the civic life of the city.

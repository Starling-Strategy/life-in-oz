Community Care and Fixing What's Broken: A Chat with Cassian Amberford and Beryl Cloverton

SYNTHETIC SOURCE — original fictional text; no real event or publication.
Format: radio_interview
Theme: Timber Verge Repair Co-op

Host:
Welcome back to Oz Community Radio, where we shine a light on the people making our corners of the world work a little better. I’m your host. Today, we’re talking about practical solutions and neighborly support. In-studio with me is Cassian Amberford, a pharmacist from Meadowbank over in Munchkin Country. And joining us via the line from Yellow Kiln is Beryl Cloverton, an appliance repairer with the Timber Verge Repair Co-op. Welcome to you both.

Cassian Amberford:
Thank you. Good to be here.

Beryl Cloverton:
Hello. Hope the line holds out better than some of the wiring I see.

Host:
Let’s hope so. Cassian, you’re our host for this segment. What’s on your mind these days, beyond the usual prescriptions and cough drops?

Cassian Amberford:
Well, it’s about those small, essential things that can slip through the cracks. I’m looking at hearing aid batteries. For a lot of folks, especially some of our older passengers, getting a fresh battery isn’t just a matter of walking into a shop. They might not have the dexterity, or the right shop might be a long walk down the Brickway. A dead battery can cut them off completely.

Host:
So what’s the plan?

Cassian Amberford:
I’m drafting a proposal to host a monthly clinic at the pharmacy. We’d bring in a technician—someone who knows the different models—to check devices, replace batteries for a small fee, just to cover costs. It’s a simple thing, but it keeps people connected. It’s still just a plan on paper, but I’ve been talking to the Reading Room Network about getting the word out when we’re ready.

Host:
A simple but vital fix. Beryl, that sounds like it’s in your wheelhouse, in a way. You fix things for a living. Can you tell us about a recent ‘simple but vital’ fix you handled?

Beryl Cloverton:
Simple isn’t always easy. Just last week, I got a call from the baker over at Hearthside, the big bakery on the west edge of Yellow Kiln. Their main deck oven was on the fritz—thermostat was lying through its teeth, saying it was hot when it was barely warm. They were about to lose the entire morning’s bread run.

Host:
A bakery without an oven is just a very fragrant room.

Beryl Cloverton:
Exactly. I got there just as the sun was coming up. Had to pull the old unit out, trace the wiring—it was a bit of a bird’s nest behind the panel—and swap in a new thermostat. Took me about two hours, start to finish. They had heat again in time to salvage the dough that was proofing. Not a single batch lost. The baker was so relieved she sent me home with a loaf still warm from the oven. That’s the best kind of payment.

Cassian Amberford:
That’s it, isn’t it? Whether it’s a thermostat or a hearing aid, the goal is to stop the interruption. To let life proceed as it’s supposed to. Your work keeps a business running. I’m just hoping to keep a few more conversations going.

Beryl Cloverton:
Same machinery, different scale. You draft your proposal, Cassian. Getting it off the page is the hard part. But if you need a metaphor about faulty wiring causing system failure for your funding request, let me know.

Cassian Amberford:
I might just take you up on that.

Host:
Cassian Amberford and Beryl Cloverton, thank you both for sharing your work. That’s all for this segment on Oz Community Radio.

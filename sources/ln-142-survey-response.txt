The following are responses from Frey Hazeldale, Community Grants Officer, to the Emerald City Leadership Network's January 2025 member survey on the Wizard's Office.

1. What does your work at the Wizard's Office involve that members might not see?

Frey Hazeldale:
A good deal of translation. Taking regulations and funding guidelines and turning them into something a person with a full-time job elsewhere can use. Last quarter, the Reading Room Network kept mentioning how their Friends of the Library volunteers were intimidated by the standard application packet. So we sat down together and made a one-page template. It just asks for the project goal, a simple budget, and a signature from the local librarian. It's not less rigorous, just clearer. We've sent it to groups in all four countries.

2. Describe a recent interaction that felt particularly productive.

Frey Hazeldale:
A theater troupe from Munchkin Country came in, quite flustered. Their grant was for a new production, and their budget narrative was a single, dense paragraph. The reviewer had flagged it as unclear. We spent an afternoon at my desk with their ledgers. We separated out lumber and hardware for set construction from bolts of cloth and dye for costumes. It wasn't about cutting costs, but about showing the thought behind each expense. They left with a document that told the story of their project, not just a list of numbers. I heard they got the funding.

3. What's one thing you wish more people knew about applying for Wizard's Office support?

Frey Hazeldale:
That the person reviewing it wants you to succeed. We're looking for reasons to fund, not to deny. A clear, honest application that shows you've thought about the details—like the difference between a saw and a sewing needle—makes our job easier and your case stronger.

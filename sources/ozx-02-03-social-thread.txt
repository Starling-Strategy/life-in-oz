Samuel Strawbridge:
Borrowed is always better than wasted. Did you have a list of who had what? In my group, one participant who usually just listens actually volunteered to read a small part again after we finished. It’s funny how the choice to participate changes the whole vibe of the room.

Tessa Finch:
I have a spreadsheet. It’s terrifying but it works. We actually did a dress rehearsal last night and it looked exactly like the village we wanted. Everyone knew which scarf went with which jacket. Since everything is borrowed, I made sure every item had a clear owner and a return date written on it, so we don’t end up with a pile of mismatched fabric in the corner.

Samuel Strawbridge:
A clear owner and a return date. That’s the kind of detail that saves you from a weekend of laundry. I’m glad the scene felt real enough for people to care about the ending.

Tessa Finch:
It really does. We’re not hiring a designer, we’re just using what we have and making it fit. It’s a lot of coordination, but it feels more like a town project than a theatrical production.

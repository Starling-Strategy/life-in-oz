Gilda Lanternmore:
To the Founders' Gala Committee,

I’m writing to nominate Xan Cinderby of Cinderby Tutoring for recognition at this year’s gala. While our work is in different fields—I manage a care home and Xan runs a tutoring center—I see a shared commitment to removing small, practical barriers so people can focus on what matters.

My own work lately has been administrative: colleagues have mentioned that a medication logbook redesign I implemented seems to be helping the pharmacy catch discrepancies a day or two earlier. It’s a quiet, paper-based fix, but it means fewer verification errors reach our residents. It’s the kind of unglamorous, systems-level thinking that makes a community function better, and that’s precisely why I admire what Xan is building.

Xan’s planned science kit lending library is a perfect example. She told me she’s sourcing simple microscopes and measurement tools for students to borrow for project work at home. For families in Bell Market who can’t afford their own equipment, this isn’t just a nice idea—it’s access. It demystifies science by putting the tools directly into kids’ hands. She’s not waiting for a grant or a perfect system; she’s exploring it now, figuring out the lending logistics and the care of the kits as she goes.

This initiative reflects the kind of practical, member-driven innovation the network celebrates. Xan identifies a need—unequal access to basic learning tools—and is crafting a community-powered solution. Her clear, structured approach turns an ambitious idea into a manageable plan. I believe her work embodies the spirit of the Founders’ Gala, and I wholeheartedly recommend her for your consideration.

Sincerely,
Gilda Lanternmore
Meadowbank Care Home

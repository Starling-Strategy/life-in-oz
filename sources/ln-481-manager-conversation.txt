Rowan Bell — community manager:
Heath Gatecombe opened the call by outlining his proposal for a pedestrian safety audit along the Brickway's commercial corridor. He noted that, while the city's general maintenance schedule exists, it doesn't always catch specific local issues. He wants a formal walkthrough to identify spots where crosswalk markings have faded or where signage is missing, to create a prioritized list for renewal. He said he'll be drafting a formal request to the Brickway Transit Union and the city works department for next month.

Heath Gatecombe:
It's about making the process visible and specific. A general 'check the paint' order gets lost. If we can say, 'The crossing at the junction by the old mill needs repainting and a new 'Yield to Pedestrians' sign,' it moves to the top of someone's list. I'm not asking for new construction, just renewal of what's supposed to be there.

Rowan Bell — community manager:

He recalled a specific, lesser-known fund earmarked for intergenerational activities. He connected them directly, and the library was able to secure the money to purchase a collection of large-print books for their weekly shared reading hours.

Tomas Saffronley:
The key was the match. The fund exists, but its criteria are quite narrow—it must bridge age groups. Their existing program fit perfectly; they just hadn't framed it that way. A simple redirect of the application to the proper channel saved them months of reapplying.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Nadia Stone described her work last week. The Southern Survey Office needed to re-map the boundary of South Copse, but the sightlines for their equipment were blocked by thick undergrowth. She spent a full day with hand shears, carefully clearing just enough brush to get clear readings without damaging the older trees. She said it was meticulous work, but by the end, the surveyor got a clean signal.

Nadia Stone:
You can't trust a reading through a tangle of willow shoots. It might give you a number, but it'll be off by three or four paces. Better to spend the day making sure it's right. We got a clear line from the red oak to the creek bend, which was the troublesome section.

Heath Gatecombe:
That's the sort of detail that makes all the difference. A faded crosswalk is a similar problem—it's a known hazard that just needs someone to point it out specifically so it can be fixed properly.

Wizard's Office Staff Look Beyond the Greenstone Gates

Theme: Wizard's Office Liaisons

While the public image of the Wizard's Office is often one of quiet corridors and paper-filled ledgers, two of its staff members are quietly reshaping how the administration connects with—and supports—the communities it serves. From site visits in the countryside to procedural reforms at a city desk, their work demonstrates a practical, ground-level focus.

Frey Hazeldale, a community grants officer, recently traded her Lantern Row office for a muddy field in Heatherford. Her mission: to assess a proposed community bread oven, a project seeking a small capital grant from the Office's civic improvement fund.

Frey Hazeldale:
The foundation work is solid. They've used good brick from the old mill, and the chimney flue is correctly sized. You can see the care.

Hazeldale spent over an hour on site, measuring the plot against the submitted plans and speaking with the residents who will manage the oven. While impressed with the construction progress, her final report included a key recommendation.

Frey Hazeldale:
The physical structure is promising, but a shared resource like this needs clear, agreed-upon protocols. Who manages the fire? What are the hours of operation? How are disputes handled? A written safety and operations plan, even a simple one, is the missing piece. It protects the grant investment and the community itself. We can't finalize the disbursement without it.

Back in the Emerald City, within the Bell Market annex of the Wizard's Office, records clerk Zora Oakholt is tackling a different kind of foundation: the bedrock of bureaucracy itself, the filing system.

Oakholt has drafted a proposal for a standardized cross-reference checksheet, aimed at complex files that touch multiple departments—think land-use permits that involve zoning, sanitation, and historic preservation.

Zora Oakholt:
The current method relies on memory, a sticky note, or a clerk's initials in a margin. It's insufficient for tracking. A misfiled multi-department document causes delays in review, delays in approvals, and delays in service. Three distinct problems.

Her proposed checksheet would live inside the file's front cover, with columns for department received, department reviewed, date out, and date returned. It's a simple tool, but one she argues would create accountability and a clear audit trail.

Zora Oakholt:
The goal is clarity, consistency, and correct placement. Three C's. It's not a revolution; it's just a better piece of paper. I've presented it to my section head, and we are reviewing it with the other annexes.

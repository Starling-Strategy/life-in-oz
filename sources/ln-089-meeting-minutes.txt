Meadow Mutual Aid Circle Meeting
Date: 12 September 2024
Present: Emrys Wickbourne, Jem Quadmore, Gilda Fernham, Phoebe Thistleholt, Saffi Wheatmore, Posy Haystead, Rowan Bell (Community Manager, non-voting).

1. Welcome and Introductions
   Rowan Bell welcomed members. The purpose of the meeting was to share updates on local aid activities, discuss resource challenges, and coordinate on upcoming seasonal needs.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Let's start with a quick round of what everyone's been up to. Posy, you mentioned something about a bake sale?

Posy Haystead:
Yes. The Bluebell Crossing group was running their annual bake sale last weekend and the old tally sheet was a mess—three people writing on the same slip, numbers getting crossed out. I drew up a new one. It's double-sided, with large numbered boxes for each item type and a column for cash and trade goods. Simple. They said it cut the confusion in half. They're going to keep using it.

2. Member Updates and Resource Sharing

Phoebe Thistleholt:
Our big project was the playground. The structures at Westgate Mills were looking rusty and wobbly. We organized a parent volunteer weekend. Got sandpaper and non-toxic paint through the Timber Verge Repair Co-op at their member rate. We had six parents out there sanding, painting, and tightening every bolt we could find. It's solid now, and the kids were back on it Monday morning.

Saffi Wheatmore:
That's wonderful. We had our own maintenance win. The playroom roof at Heatherford has had a slow leak for months, right over the block corner. We finally had a parent volunteer who used to work with roofing materials. I supervised—mostly just held the ladder and made tea. She patched it beautifully with some salvaged roofing felt from an old shed repair. Got it done just before the last big rain. Dry as a bone now.

Gilda Fernham:
It's the season for using what's at hand. I was asked to do the centrepieces for the Winkie harvest dinner—twenty tables. I didn't want to use floral foam, so I designed arrangements using dried native grasses. We dyed some with beet and onion skin for colour, arranged them in borrowed ceramic bowls. They were a hit. After the dinner, the whole lot went straight into the community compost heap. Nothing wasted.

Emrys Wickbourne:
Oh, I love that! So tactile. I've been wrestling with supply chains, which is less poetic. I had an order for a specialty fleece for a puppet set—a big, shaggy monster commission. My usual supplier's invoice system completely failed. They couldn't process payment for a week. I had to wait it out, which pushed the whole project back by a week. The client was understanding, but it meant my workshop schedule was a juggling act. It's done now, the monster is very shaggy.

3. Tenant Support Discussion
   Jem Quadmore provided an overview of common issues facing tenants in Bluebell Crossing as the colder season approaches.

Jem Quadmore:
The main thing right now is getting people prepared for the heating season and knowing their rights about repairs. It's about filling out forms correctly and knowing which civic office to bother. Speaking of forms...

Rowan Bell — community manager:
There was a mention at the last Reading Room meet-up that you'd helped someone outside your own building with a particularly complicated housing repair grant application. Is that something you'd advise others on?

Jem Quadmore:
(Pauses) Well, I don't know who said that. But the application for the Civic Housing Repair Grant is a beast. It's eight pages and asks for three different proofs of residency. If a tenant in another building asked for a hand, I wouldn't say no. It's just paperwork. The key is getting the dates lined up with the inspection report and not skipping the supplemental budget sheet. That's where most people get tripped up.

4. Seasonal Planning and Coordination
   The group discussed priorities for the coming months: winter coat and blanket drives, holiday meal coordination, and checking in on elderly or isolated neighbors.

Gilda Fernham:
The dried grass centrepieces were one idea for low-waste gatherings. I'm happy to share the simple dye method if any other community dinners are planned.

Posy Haystead:
I can adapt the tally sheet template for a coat drive inventory. It would track sizes and conditions easily.

Saffi Wheatmore:
Heatherford can host a collection point. We have the space in the co-op's front hall.

Phoebe Thistleholt:
Westgate Mills will promote it through our parent network.

Emrys Wickbourne:
I might have some fabric scraps that could be used for patching or small quilting projects. I'll sort through my off-cuts.

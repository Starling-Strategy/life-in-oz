Rowan Bell — community manager:
Rowan Bell here. Debrief from the March Winkie Works Guild monthly meeting, held at the Guildhall on the 15th. Good turnout, good energy. Notes follow.

The meeting opened with updates on work done by members since February.

Tilda Poppyley:
Green Market Reading Room. Archive shelves were sagging. Brackets were weak. I put in steel gussets. Cut them myself from one sheet. They’re solid now.

Hugo Peppermere:
I organized a tool sharpening clinic for our carpentry apprentices last week. We had three experienced carpenters from the Brickway Union demonstrate proper technique for chisels and plane irons. The focus was on maintenance, not just sharpening. It was well-received.

Hattie Violetholt:
At Westgate Foundry, we addressed the scrap metal sorting bottleneck. I sourced three heavy-duty bins and labeled them by alloy type: ferrous, non-ferrous copper-base, non-ferrous aluminum-base. I then created and laminated a simple identification guide, posted it on the wall by the scale. The crew reports it’s reduced sorting time by about fifteen minutes per load.

Coralie Stonecombe:
Following our last safety walk-through, I noted several workshop first-aid kits were low on supplies. I completed a full inventory across all guild-affiliated shops this month. I’ve now replenished the adhesive bandages, antiseptic wipes, and burn gel in every kit. The standard is now restored.

The floor then opened for discussion of upcoming ideas and concerns.

Iolo Winkleton:
We’re losing too many reusable pallets between shipping and receiving. It’s a waste. I want to propose a simple system: paint a number on each pallet in our yard, keep a chalkboard log. Ship out, check it off when it comes back. It’s not complicated. I’ll draft something for the next meeting.

Hattie Violetholt:
Iolo’s right, we’ve been treating them as disposable. A tracking system would help. I’d want to see the draft.

Hugo Peppermere:
That’s a practical initiative. Perhaps we could involve an apprentice in maintaining the log. It would be good training in inventory control. Let’s see Iolo’s proposal next month.

Tilda Poppyley:
Numbers wear off. Stencil them, use a hard paint. Metal tag might be better.

Iolo Winkleton:
I’ll look at both. The tag might last longer.

Other business included a reminder about the upcoming Guild-wide safety refresher in April and a brief discussion on rising material costs. No resolutions, but members shared sources for bulk orders.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Actions and notes for next time: Iolo to draft the pallet tracking proposal. Hugo and Coralie to coordinate on including an apprentice in the system if it’s approved. Rowan to circulate the safety refresher schedule. Next meeting: April 19th.

Brickway Transit Union Joint Committee Meeting
Date: 5 December 2024
Present: Greer Saffroncombe (Driver), Kerensa Hedgecombe (Works Foreman), Bertram Violetridge (Depot Mechanic), Jem Haymere (Depot Mechanic), Orla Amberholt (Works Foreman), Rowan Bell (Community Manager, Emerald City Leadership Network)

1. WINTER PREPAREDNESS AND FLEET STATUS
   Bertram Violetridge reported on the heating system overhaul project. He led the team in replacing cores and flushing lines on eight of the older model buses. The work was completed ahead of the first frost, and all eight vehicles are now in the regular winter rotation with no heating failures reported to date.

2. ON-ROAD MAINTENANCE AND EMERGENCY RESPONSE
   Jem Haymere detailed a critical intervention during the late summer heatwave. A bus scheduled for the long Winkie route showed signs of imminent overheating during its pre-trip inspection. Jem performed an emergency radiator flush and cleaning on the spot, which took approximately ninety minutes. The bus departed on schedule and completed its route without incident.

3. BRICKWAY AND ACCESS ROAD REPAIRS
   Kerensa Hedgecombe reported on a materials reclamation project in Yellow Kiln. Her crew salvaged several dozen usable cobblestones from a demolished courtyard. These stones were later used to patch a worn section of pedestrian pathway adjacent to the Brickway Works depot, creating a more stable walking surface for crew and public.

Orla Amberholt reported on a recent pothole repair on a secondary access road near Redfern Hill. Her two-person crew patched a deep pothole using a pre-mixed cold asphalt compound. They completed the work, including manual compaction with a tamping tool, before the morning traffic peak, restoring safe passage for local and service vehicles.

4. SERVICE AND COMMUNITY OBSERVATIONS
   Anecdotal feedback from passengers was noted. Greer Saffroncombe mentioned that other drivers have reported seeing a small, wrapped tin of biscuits left on a bus seat on several Tuesday mornings, apparently for a regular elderly passenger who rides to a clinic. The origin of the gesture was not confirmed, but it was noted as a kind, quiet practice.

Kerensa Hedgecombe:
Those salvaged stones are solid. Better than the crushed aggregate we’d have ordered, and it kept them out of the landfill pile. The pathway patch should last years.

Bertram Violetridge:
The heating work was straightforward but time-consuming. The key was getting all eight done before the cold set in. We’ve scheduled a check on the seals in another month.

Jem Haymere:
The radiator was nearly clogged. If it had gone out on the Winkie route, the tow and repair would have taken the bus out for two days, minimum. The flush was the correct procedure.

Orla Amberholt:
Cold patch at that depth isn’t ideal, but it was what we had and it got the road safe. We’ve flagged the section for a full hot-mix repair in the spring works schedule.

Greer Saffroncombe:
It’s a long ride to the clinic. A small thing can make a difference. I can’t speak to who left the biscuits, but it’s the sort of thing that reminds you we’re moving people, not just parcels.

5. OPEN ACTIONS AND NEXT STEPS
   - Bertram Violetridge will monitor the performance of the overhauled heating systems and report any issues at the next meeting.
   - Orla Amberholt will submit the location and dimensions of the patched pothole for inclusion in the spring permanent repair schedule.
   - All members are to report any observed pedestrian pathway wear or trip hazards near depots and major stops to Kerensa Hedgecombe’s crew.
   - The committee will reconvene on 6 February 2025 to review mid-winter status.

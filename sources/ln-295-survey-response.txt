Brickway Transit Union Member Survey Responses, July 2025

SYNTHETIC SOURCE — original fictional text; no real event or publication.
Format: survey_response
Theme: Brickway Transit Union

Survey Question 1: In your own words, what's the best thing you or your team accomplished in the last month?

Mina Vale:
Oh, I don't know about accomplishments, but I heard a little rumor floating around the depot. Some of the other drivers were talking about the Brickway terminus, how folks were having to wait right on the curb because there's no place to sit. I might have mentioned that if we shifted the schedule board just a few feet toward the station house, there'd be a perfect patch of level ground for a bench. Wouldn't cost much, and my regulars—especially the older passengers—would really appreciate it. It's just a thought I passed on.

Elena Cross:
The evening peak on Route 12 was getting bunched up. We had drivers reporting back-to-back, then a long gap. I got on the radio and instructed everyone on that route to use the designated layover point at the Meadow Mutual Aid hall if they were running less than eight minutes behind the schedule ahead of them. It took a couple of days for the rhythm to settle, but it smoothed out the service. The number of complaints about long waits followed by full buses dropped noticeably by the end of the week.

Victor Sedge:
We had a blocked culvert under the Brickway, just west of the junction. Standing water was threatening to undermine the fresh asphalt on the newly repaved section. I supervised a crew of three. We used long-handled rakes to pull out the bulk of the debris—leaves, some old cloth—and then a rented suction pump to clear the silt that was packed in deep. Took us the better part of a day, but we got it flowing freely. The roadbed stayed dry.

Survey Question 2: What's one thing the network could do to better support the work you do?

Mina Vale:
Sometimes the chatter between circles is lovely, but it can get a bit abstract. It'd be nice to have a simple way to share very practical, small-scale problems and solutions. Like, 'Here's how our circle fixed a wobbly signpost,' or 'We found a supplier for durable bench slats.' Less about big plans, more about the little fixes that make the day-to-day smoother.

Elena Cross:
A shared calendar of major road works, even just the ones planned by the Works Guild and the Repair Co-op, would be invaluable. If I know a key artery is going to be down for a day, I can re-route drivers proactively instead of reacting to the jam. It doesn't have to be overly detailed, just dates and affected stretches.

Victor Sedge:
Connecting with the growers or the timber co-op about material sourcing. We're always looking for good, local gravel for patch jobs, or sturdy wood for temporary signage and barrier frames. If there's a network member who produces something like that, it'd be good to know. Keeps the work and the money circulating here.

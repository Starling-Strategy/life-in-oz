Dear colleagues,

I trust this finds you well. It has been a rather routine week here at the shop, though I did host our little fountain pen maintenance demonstration on Saturday last. A modest crowd of fifteen turned up, which was just right for the space. We covered the proper cleaning of nibs and the importance of using a good, non-clogging ink. I was pleasantly surprised to see several attendees later purchase some of our finer bottled inks—the ones from the Gillikin glassworks. It seems a bit of practical knowledge still encourages a bit of practical shopping.

I heard a lovely snippet from Ewan Meadowton about his work with the old menus, which sounds fascinating. And Ren, I must say, your description of the new lighting in your back aisle gave me an idea for my own stockroom. Might you share the name of your supplier?

Warmly,
Ursa

Hello Ursa, Ren, all,

Thank you for the kind words about the menu project. It was quite a task, but a pleasant one. I managed to compile and describe fifty handwritten menus from eateries that once thrived in the old Bell Market. You can see the shift in popular dishes—the rise of the spiced root bake in the late sixties, for instance—and of course, the steady creep of prices. A cup of tea for two coppers! It’s a slice of social history, really, told through supper specials.

Ursa, your pen demonstration sounds wonderful. The care of good tools is a theme that resonates across all trades, doesn’t it? And Ren, better lighting is never a small thing. I’ve spent too many hours in dim archives to disagree.

Best,
Ewan

Post-Festival Committee Debrief – June 2026

SYNTHETIC SOURCE — original fictional text; no real event or publication.
Format: post_event_debrief
Theme: Festival Committee

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Rowan Bell, notes. Meeting of the Festival Committee, follow-up on Lantern Row festival and other member-led projects. Present: Oriel Emerystead, Dacey Brambleley, Milo Winkleham, Aurelia Ashmere.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
General feeling was that the Lantern Row festival itself went smoothly, good turnout, but the focus here is on the work members did around the edges.

Oriel reported on the music swap. They’d been sitting on a stack of duplicate brass band marches—'taking up space and gathering dust,' they said—and reached out to the Emerald City Municipal Orchestra’s librarian.

Oriel Emerystead:
It was like a key change in the middle of a tune. We had all this redundant fanfare, they had these lovely, simpler concert pieces sitting unused. Swapped a dozen marches for about twenty pieces. The beginner-level stuff is perfect for our youth ensemble—gives them a real repertoire to grow into, not just exercises.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Oriel has already distributed the new sheets to the band. The swap is done; the orchestra librarian was apparently delighted.

Milo gave a succinct report on the Lantern Row lighting. The decorative street lamps along the main route use old-fashioned filament bulbs, and a significant number had blown out over the last year.

Milo Winkleham:
Sixty-three bulbs replaced. I checked every socket first for corrosion or loose fittings—found about five that needed a quick clean with contact spray. The festival budget covered the bulbs, but the labor was mine. It’s just brighter now. Aesthetic, but also a safety thing.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Milo confirmed all replacements are complete and working. The brighter lighting was noted and appreciated by several neighbors during the festival.

Dacey Brambleley brought up an ongoing project. The Timber Verge Repair Co-op’s workshop has a collection of specialized, oddly-shaped wrenches and calipers that currently live in a jumble in a drawer.

Dacey Brambleley:
It’s a mess. Tools get damaged, people waste time digging. I offered to design a proper wall-mounted rack for them. Not just pegs—custom holders that fit the contours of each tool type. I’ll draft it up, send the plans to their shop lead. If they like it, they can build it themselves or I can quote the fabrication.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
This is an offer, not a completed action. Dacey will draft the design and share it. No commitment from the Co-op yet, but the offer stands.

Aurelia Ashmere updated the group on her library work outside the festival scope.

Aurelia Ashmere:
I’m in the early stages of drafting a grant application. The goal is to fund a new graphic novel collection specifically for reluctant readers at our school. We have the space on the shelves, and I’ve compiled a list of titles that have worked wonders in other districts. It’s about meeting the students where they are, visually. The application is due in the fall, so I’m gathering supporting data now.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
This is a plan in progress. Aurelia is drafting the application; no funding has been secured.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Open actions: Dacey to share tool rack designs with Timber Verge Co-op by mid-July. Aurelia to circulate a draft of her grant application to the committee for feedback if she wishes. No other pending actions from this meeting.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Next check-in scheduled for September, unless the tool rack project moves faster.

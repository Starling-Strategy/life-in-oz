FOR RELEASE
November 2025

To the Emerald City Small Business Sustainability Fund,

This report details the use of funds awarded to The Greenfield Table, Junction Quarter, for equipment maintenance and waste reduction initiatives. The grant has supported operational resilience and environmental stewardship.

In late October, Otis Greenfield identified a high-pitched whine and insufficient cooling from the restaurant's commercial ice maker. Suspecting a mechanical fault, he removed the rear panel—a task he noted was simpler than expected—and observed the condenser fan was spinning irregularly. By diagnosing the specific failed component himself, he was able to order the exact replacement motor and fan assembly from a supplier in the Winkie Country, avoiding a full service call from a commercial repair company. The part cost approximately one-fifth of a service estimate. He installed it successfully, restoring the unit to full operation within three days. This proactive maintenance prevented a potential complete failure during a busy weekend, ensuring continuous service for both dining and bar patrons.

Otis Greenfield:
I've always believed in knowing how your own equipment works. That whine wasn't right, and getting in there showed me exactly what we needed. It saved us time, money, and a lot of melted ice.

Separately, with a portion of the grant earmarked for waste reduction, The Greenfield Table launched a reusable takeout container program in early November. Customers may now opt for a sturdy, lidded bamboo container for a two-coin deposit, refundable upon its return. Initial uptake has been strong, particularly among regulars from the nearby Reading Room and Brickway Transit offices. Over the first three weeks, the restaurant has seen a reduction in single-use packaging waste by approximately one-third. A small shelf near the entrance facilitates easy returns. The program has also sparked conversations with customers about other ways to reduce waste, creating a minor but positive community ripple effect.

Otis Greenfield:
People were ready for it. They like the feel of the boxes—they're solid, they don't leak. And it feels good to see fewer of those paper cartons going out the door every night. It's a start.

Funds were used for the purchase of the condenser fan assembly and fifty initial bamboo containers with labels. The project remains ongoing; tracking of container circulation and long-term waste metrics will continue. The Greenfield Table extends its thanks for the support, which has directly enhanced both the business's sustainability and its operational self-reliance.

Respectfully submitted,
Otis Greenfield
The Greenfield Table

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Called Barnaby Haybourne for a monthly check-in on Gillikin Growers activities. He was in high spirits, having just come in from checking soil samples.

Barnaby Haybourne:
Rowan! Just the thing. I've been up to my elbows in dirt for the city's new flowerbed project. They had six possible spots, and I tested every one. The pH tells you everything—two were far too acidic, one was practically alkaline. But I found three beauties along the south Brickway stretch: good drainage, neutral pH. You don't need to amend the soil much at all, which saves the project committee a bundle on lime and compost. I sent the report over yesterday.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Noted. Any other projects on the horizon?

Barnaby Haybourne:
Oh, it's all about planning ahead. I'm thinking autumn. The city's parks shed leaves like nobody's business, and they just send them off to be composted centrally. What a waste of a perfect resource! I want to organize a proper leaf collection drive—get volunteers to bag them up right there in the parks. Then we store the bags over winter, and come spring, gardeners from all the circles can have leaf mould for their plots. It's a year-long process, but if we start the organizing now, we'll be ready when the first gingko drops.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
So that's still in the planning phase? No dates set yet?

Barnaby Haybourne:
Exactly right. It's an intention, an offer. I need to talk to the park maintenance folks and the Chamber circle to see about permissions and storage. But the science is sound. Leaf mould is gold.

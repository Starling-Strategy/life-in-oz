Rowan Bell · Community Manager · call with Emrys Crowham and Gilda Lanternmore · April 2025

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Emrys opened by describing a small but persistent issue at South Copse. The dining room door had begun sticking against the threshold, making it very difficult for residents using walkers to open it on their own. He said he took an afternoon last week to plane down the bottom edge and reapply a fresh sealant strip. He mentioned the difference was immediate—less strain, more independence.

Emrys Crowham:
It’s one of those things you walk past for a week thinking ‘I must get to that,’ but it’s the accumulation of those small barriers that wears on people. Having it swing freely again… you could see the relief.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Gilda shared something from Meadowbank. She said she’d been talking with a resident who missed her garden, which got her thinking. She reached out to a market gardener who sets up near the care home and arranged a weekly rotation of fresh, seasonal blooms.

Gilda Lanternmore:
It’s not a program, just a small delivery. We put little vases in the common areas, the front desk, the library nook. The colors… you notice people stopping to look. It’s a quiet thing, but it seems to lift the atmosphere.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Both emphasized these weren’t major initiatives, just part of the daily work of noticing what’s needed. Emrys said he’s keeping an eye on two other doors that might need the same attention before the humid season. Gilda mentioned the gardener is happy with the steady, small order and may offer a similar arrangement to other homes in the network if there’s interest.

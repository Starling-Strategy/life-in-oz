FOR RELEASE: September 2026, Blue Orchard, Munchkin Country

This report details the use of funds from the Emerald City Leadership Network’s Small Projects Grant, awarded in August 2026 to support the Blue Orchard community fridge. The grant was used to establish a reliable source of staple foods and to improve user education and safety.

Juniper Ashfield:
The main thing was getting a steady stream of baked goods. I talked with the folks at the Blue Orchard bakery on Orchard Lane. They were already looking for a way to handle their day-old items that didn’t feel wasteful. Now, on Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Saturdays, I swing by after they close. They’ve got a box ready for me with bread, rolls, sometimes muffins or pastries that are perfectly fine, just from the day before. It means the fridge almost always has something carby and filling, which people really look for.

This arrangement, secured by Juniper Ashfield, began in early September. The bakery does not charge for the goods, and the grant funds covered the cost of reusable storage containers and fuel for the weekly pickups. The supply has been consistent, with an average of three full containers delivered each week.

Juniper Ashfield:
The other part was the sign. I noticed some confusion—people leaving things that shouldn’t be there, or taking more than their share on a tough day. The old note was faded. I used some of the grant to get a proper, weatherproof placard made. It’s got simple pictures and clear words: ‘Take what you need,’ ‘Check dates on dairy and meat,’ ‘Please, no homemade meals or opened packages.’ I put it right on the fridge door at eye level.

The sign was installed in mid-September. While direct measurement of behavioral change is ongoing, Juniper reports fewer inappropriate donations and less frequent over-clearing of the fridge shelves since its placement.

Juniper Ashfield:
It’s not perfect, but it’s better. You still get the occasional bunch of homegrown zucchini, which is fine, or someone who forgets. But most folks see the sign and follow it. The bread pickup is the real win, though. It’s something people can count on.

Grant funds are now fully expended. The ongoing costs for the bread pickup (transport) will be absorbed by Juniper Ashfield personally. The sign is a permanent fixture. Future needs include potential repairs to the fridge’s sealing gasket and a desire for a secondary, covered shelf for non-perishable items, but these are not currently funded.

Submitted by Juniper Ashfield, Blue Orchard Community Fridge Keeper, on behalf of the Emerald City Leadership Network.

Hello everyone,

A couple of things from this week.

First, the basic plumbing tools workshop went well. We had a full house—mostly folks who'd never held a pipe wrench before. I brought in some old fixtures from the salvage pile and we practiced on those. I showed how to tell a basin wrench from a standard one, and how to get a good grip without stripping the nut. Since then, three of the attendees have signed out wrenches and drain snakes to tackle small jobs at home. It's good to see the tools getting used right.

Second, Gideon finished that sewing machine case for the mobile mending service. He showed it to me yesterday. It's plywood, but he's lined it with a fitted foam padding he cut himself, so the machine sits snug and won't get jostled on those rural roads. The handles are sturdy leather. It looks like it'll last for years. They picked it up this morning, very pleased.

All the best,
Pia

The case was a good project. I used half-inch plywood for the shell and a closed-cell foam for the padding. It needed to be light enough to carry but rigid enough to protect the machine. I routed out channels for the foam so it locks in place. The service coordinator said it’ll make their weekly circuit much easier.

Your workshop sounds useful. I’ve been thinking about a session on maintaining hand-crank machines. If there’s interest.

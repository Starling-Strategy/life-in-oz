Small Moves, Big Days: Festival Committee Members Make It Work

Format: news_article
Theme: Festival Committee

While the Emerald City Leadership Network’s Festival Committee is best known for orchestrating large-scale public celebrations, the work that sustains those events—and the members themselves—often happens in quieter moments. This month, three members from across Oz demonstrated that the skills needed to pull off a festival are the same ones that keep communities running day to day.

In Reed Flats, Flora Hayworth of Hayworth Catering recently navigated the unique challenges of a Munchkin Country barn wedding for three hundred guests. The rustic venue lacked a permanent kitchen, so Flora orchestrated the temporary installation of a field kitchen, borrowed from a neighboring farm cooperative.

Flora Hayworth:
You make do with what you have, and you borrow what you don't. The trick is knowing who has a propane burner large enough to simmer four pots of stew at once. We had to run lines from the main farmhouse, but it worked a treat. By the end, the barn was full of good smells and full plates, which is all anyone really wants.

Meanwhile, in Gillikin Country, events producer Anselm Ashdale is already looking ahead. As a key planner for the Violet Hills open-air book market, a staple of the autumn festival season, he’s growing concerned about weather patterns.

Anselm Ashdale:
The last two years, we’ve had to scramble. A beautiful morning can turn into a downpour by noon, and then you have a river of runoff threatening centuries-old books and very frustrated booksellers. My intention for this summer is to personally visit every suitable indoor space within a ten-minute walk of the square—church halls, empty shop fronts, the community center. We need a rain plan that’s a real plan, not a prayer.

Anselm’s scouting mission, while still in the planning stages, reflects a proactive approach to preserving the event’s charm against increasingly unpredictable skies.

Also in Gillikin, Alma Reedton of the Heatherford Brass Band attended to a smaller, but no less critical, piece of festival infrastructure: the band’s gear. The heavy canvas bag used to transport folding music stands had finally given way at the seams after years of service.

Alma Reedton:
It was a clean tear, but it ran along the stress points. I could have just patched it, but I wanted it to be stronger than before. I used a proper waxed thread—the kind used for sailcloth—and doubled the stitching along all the seams. It took an evening, but now it won’t fail when we’re halfway across the Quadling Country on the festival circuit. The stands are safe, and that means the music goes on.

From feeding crowds and fortifying venues to mending the tools of the trade, these episodes underscore a shared ethos: whether the scale is three hundred guests or one canvas bag, thoughtful preparation is what turns potential chaos into celebration.

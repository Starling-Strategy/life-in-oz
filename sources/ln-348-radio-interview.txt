Brickway Junction Dispatch: Keeping the Road Smooth

SYNTHETIC SOURCE — original fictional text; no real event or publication.
Format: radio_interview
Theme: Brickway Works

Host:
Welcome back to Munchkinland AM, your community voice on the dial. With summer festivals winding down, many of us are thinking about the roads that got us there and back. Today, we're joined by Victor Sedge, Road Maintenance Lead with Brickway Works. Victor, thanks for coming in.

Victor Sedge:
Appreciate the invitation. It's a busy season, so it's good to talk.

Host:
Let's start with the basics for our listeners. Brickway Works maintains that critical stretch from the Junction out toward the city. What's a typical day look like for your crews?

Victor Sedge:
Typical is a four-man day crew and a three-man night crew. The day handles the heavier repairs, inspections, coordinating with the transit union. The night crew does the cleaning, the sweeping, the urgent patch jobs that are easier without cart traffic. The handoff between shifts is where things used to get... knotted up.

Host:
Knotted up how?

Victor Sedge:
Tools. We share equipment between shifts—tampers, the concrete saw, good shovels. A night guy would come in, need the tamper, and it'd be gone. Maybe the day crew left it on a truck, maybe it walked off. Lots of 'he said, she said' and lost time. So I put a logbook on a nail by the depot door.

Host:
A logbook. Simple as that?

Victor Sedge:
Simple as that. Columns for date, tool, crew, time out, time in. Now, if the tamper's not on the rack, you check the book. It's with the day crew on mile marker seven. Or it's signed out to the night shift for a repair at the crossing. No arguments. It's cut the morning chatter about missing gear by, I'd say, ninety percent. Lets us start the actual work.

Host:
That's a tangible fix. Now, speaking of repairs, I understand you had a significant push before the festival traffic hit. Can you tell us about that?

Victor Sedge:
Sure. We'd cataloged over thirty reported potholes on the Brickway approach—that's the last two miles before you hit the city limits. Cart axles, cycle tires... it was getting rough. With the summer festival surge coming, we couldn't let that stand. So I coordinated a weekend blitz.

Host:
A blitz meaning...

Victor Sedge:
Meaning I got both my day crew and the night crew to come in on a Saturday. Two crews, seven men total. We used a pre-mixed cold patch material—good for quick turnaround—and just worked down the list. One crew prepping and filling, the other compacting and moving barricades. Started at dawn, finished by mid-afternoon.

Host:
All thirty holes?

Victor Sedge:
All thirty-two we had marked by that morning. The cold patch sets up firm enough for light traffic immediately, which was the point. It's not a permanent fix for every one, but it got the road smooth for the festival carts and coaches. Prevented a lot of shaken-up passengers and delayed goods.

Host:
Victor Sedge, Road Maintenance Lead for Brickway Works. Thanks for sharing a bit of what keeps our main artery moving.

Victor Sedge:
Thank you. Just remember, if you see something on the Brickway, report it to the Junction depot. We're listening.

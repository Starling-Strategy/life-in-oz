Bell Market Radio: Mapping the Lanes with Hannah Bellamy

SYNTHETIC SOURCE — original fictional text; no real event or publication.
Format: radio_interview
Theme: Bell Market community radio

Felix Rowe:
And we're back on the air, Bell Market! This is Felix Rowe, your host and producer, and today we're talking about getting things where they need to go, especially when the main roads are… well, you know how they are. My guest is Hannah Bellamy, program director over at the Brickway Junction Community Centre. Hannah, welcome to the station!

Hannah Bellamy:
Thanks, Felix! It's great to be here. And you're right about the roads—sometimes the best route isn't the one painted on the sign.

Felix Rowe:
Exactly. Now, before you were running programs, you were on a bicycle, delivering all over the Munchkin country side. How did that turn into the map the centre uses now?

Hannah Bellamy:
Oh, it was just necessity! When I was a courier, you learn the alleys, the cut-throughs, which garden gates are never locked—the safe, quick ways that the delivery services' fancy grids never show. When I started at the centre, we were trying to get meals and library books to older folks, and the drivers kept hitting dead ends. So I sat down with a big piece of paper and just… drew it. All the back lanes from the Brickway out to the Meadow fields. The crew from Meadow Mutual Aid helped test it, marking where a cart can't fit or where the cobbles are too rough. Now every volunteer driver gets a copy. It's not pretty, but it gets the bread delivered.

Felix Rowe:
A hand-drawn map that beats the official one. I love it. And that's just one piece. I hear the centre itself is now open later?

Hannah Bellamy:
Yes! That's the new evening program. We kept hearing from parents and from the transit union folks whose shifts end late: 'If only we had somewhere to go that wasn't a tavern or home.' So, three nights a week now, the main hall is open. No formal agenda. There's tea, the reading room corner is lit, there's space for a repair co-op member to fix a lamp, or for someone from the health access circle to just be around to chat. Last week, a road crew member came in straight from a night job, warmed up, and ended up helping a neighbor re-stitch a bootsole. That's the idea—just a warm, lit place.

Felix Rowe:
A warm, lit place. Sounds simple, but we all know how rare that can be after dark. How did you get the lights to stay on, so to speak?

Hannah Bellamy:
Oh, it's everyone. The Timber Verge folks checked all our windows and doors to make sure no heat was leaking. The Gillikin Growers circle brings extra apples for the table. And our own volunteers—mostly people who use the centre during the day—take turns staying late. It's not a big production. We just… unlocked the doors later.

Felix Rowe:
Unlocking the doors later. Hannah Bellamy, program director at the Brickway Junction Community Centre. Thank you for coming in and sharing the map—and for keeping the lights on. We'll be right back after this.

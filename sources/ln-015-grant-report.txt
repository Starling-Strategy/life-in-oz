FOR RELEASE: February 2024, Emerald City, Oz

Grant funds awarded to the Emerald City Chamber in late 2023 were allocated to support the operational resilience of local retail members, with a focus on navigating unforeseen disruptions and fostering collaborative problem-solving. This report outlines the use of funds and related member-led initiatives during the period.

Florence Gray of Gray's Grocery in Bell Market utilized the Chamber's small-business contingency advisory to manage a three-day closure of her dairy aisle after a cooler unit's slow leak was discovered. She transferred perishables to insulated crates, preventing total loss, and successfully negotiated a partial credit from the repair service to offset the lost trade. "You can't ledger a melted cheese," Gray noted, "but you can ledger the conversation that gets you a credit for it."

Zeb Lanternham, Chamber Membership Officer, organized a forum for over twenty small retailers to meet directly with the city's contracted commercial waste collection service. Persistent concerns about inconsistent pick-up times were aired, resulting in the contractor agreeing to a revised, more reliable schedule for three central districts. "The goal was a direct line, not just another complaint filed into the void," Lanternham said. "We got the schedule amended, which is a concrete start."

Ren Hazelham of Hazelham Hardware in Lantern Row provided an exemplary case of hyper-local inventory and knowledge. A customer arrived with an unusual, fin-shaped cabinet key that no modern supplier carried. Hazelham retrieved a match from a box of unlabeled vintage hardware he keeps under the counter, solving a problem that had stalled the customer's furniture restoration for weeks. This kind of service, while not directly funded by the grant, exemplifies the adaptive capacity the Chamber seeks to foster.

Grant expenditures were primarily directed toward securing the venue and administrative coordination for the waste collection forum, as well as covering the cost of a shared refrigeration consultant who provided the initial assessment for Gray's Grocery. Remaining funds are earmarked for compiling a resource directory of vetted repair and service providers, a project currently in the planning stages with several Chamber members volunteering to contribute reviews.

The interconnectedness of these events—from a hardware shop's niche inventory to a grocer's salvage operation to collective bargaining on municipal services—illustrates the Chamber's role in facilitating both individual resilience and shared advocacy. The work is ongoing and fundamentally practical.

About: The Emerald City Chamber is a circle within the Emerald City Leadership Network, supporting local commerce and enterprise through connection, resource-sharing, and advocacy.

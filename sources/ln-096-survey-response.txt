Emerald City Leadership Network | Members' Survey (October 2024)

Respondent: Yara Meadowby, Meadowby Stationers

Question 1: In the last few months, have you started or completed a project that contributes to your local community or your professional circle? If so, please give us a short description.

Yara Meadowby:
Two things, really. The first was a little salvage project. I was reorganizing the cellar and found an old metal postcard rack, terribly corroded and wobbly. It took me a week of evenings—cleaning off the rust, stabilizing the joints, polishing it up. It's quite a handsome piece now, and I've put it up front by the window. It displays postcards and small prints from a handful of local artists on a consignment basis. It's nice to give their work a spot that isn't just a crowded rack by the register.

Yara Meadowby:
The other was the fountain pen clinic. I hosted a free drop-in afternoon here in the shop. I set out some tools—ultrasonic cleaners, flush bulbs, my little loupe—and helped people clean out their clogged pens. I think twelve people came through? I demonstrated how to do a basic disassembly and cleaning safely. It was lovely. People were so pleased to have a working pen again, especially the older ones with sentimental value. One gentleman’s pen hadn't written since his school days.

Question 2: What kind of network support or connections would be most useful to you right now?

Yara Meadowby:
I'm always interested in connecting with more local artists and artisans, perhaps through the Reading Room Network or the Festival Committee, to keep the rack fresh. Also, if any members have experience with small-scale retail consignment agreements, I'd appreciate a chance to compare notes. Mine is very simple, but I want to be sure it's fair.

Question 3: Is there anything else you'd like the network to know about your work or your neighborhood?

Yara Meadowby:
Spectacle Lane remains a quiet, bookish corner. We get a lovely mix of students, archivists from the City Library, and people just seeking a bit of calm. The pen clinic reminded me how much people value places where they can fix things instead of replace them. I'd like to explore more of that—maybe a basic bookbinding repair demo next? I'm open to ideas.

Small Business Circle: Speaker Bios for October 2025 Panel

Cosmo Heatherfield has run Heatherfield's Barbers in Reed Flats for more years than he cares to count. He's known for a dry chuckle and a practical approach to the nuts and bolts of running a shop. Seeing his colleagues each paying separately for sharpening their shears, he's now proposing a collective solution: a monthly bulk run to a Winkie tool specialist to bring down the cost for every barber on the street.

Mabel Brambleshaw of Brambleshaw Haulage has driven the route between Blue Orchard and the Emerald City more times than most people have had hot dinners. Her voice carries the direct, road-weary wisdom of someone who knows that official maps don't always tell the full story. To keep her runs on schedule, she recently created a detailed, taped-together paper map of every known detour and rough-road section, which now lives, neatly folded, in her cab for daily reference.

Fergus Bramblemore, owner of Bramblemore Laundry in Reed Flats, speaks in a weathered but steady tone, always grounding his ideas in concrete examples. Faced with the constant need for packaging, he looked no further than his neighbor. He arranged a trade with the local bakery, laundering their aprons and kitchen linens in exchange for a monthly allotment of their sturdy flour sacks, which he now cleans and repurposes for his own deliveries.

Rhoda Reedshaw runs Reedshaw Laundry in Hammerhead Rise with a no-nonsense efficiency warmed by a laugh that puts people at ease. She believes in fixing what you have before you replace it. With that in mind, she plans to offer a free, basic linen mending workshop for the staff at local care homes and small hotels, focusing on simple repairs like seams and small tears to extend the working life of their sheets, towels, and tablecloths.

FOR RELEASE: August 2024

To the Oz Community Fund,

This report summarizes the use of the grant awarded in June 2024 to support the Meadow Mutual Aid network's initiatives aimed at reducing small, practical burdens for residents. The funds were allocated across three counties and have supported projects in housing, healthcare, and small business continuity. The overarching theme of the work has been addressing logistical friction before it becomes a crisis.

In Quadling Country, the grant supported community-building efforts at the South Meadow Housing Cooperative. Treasurer Elias Moss reports that conversations facilitated by the grant led to a new proposal currently under discussion. Co-op members have mentioned that Moss floated the idea of offering a small rent credit to residents who volunteer to assist with the cooperative's monthly building inspections. This initiative, still in the planning phase, aims to distribute the workload of maintaining their older building more equitably while providing direct, tangible relief to participating members. Moss notes, 'It's about recognizing the labor. A credit against next month's rent is a clear, immediate value for someone giving up a Saturday morning. We're working out the math to make it sustainable.'

Caleb Alder:
At the North Orchard Clinic in Gillikin Country, the funds were used for a direct patient-support project. Clinic reception lead Caleb Alder identified a recurring issue: patients with portable medical devices, like oxygen monitors or insulin pumps, often found their batteries depleted during long wait times. 'You'd see people anxiously watching their device blink, worried more about the battery than their appointment,' Alder said. He used the grant to purchase a sturdy power strip and a set of labeled, color-coded charging cables. These were installed at a dedicated table in the waiting room, clearly marked for patient use. The result has been a small but noticeable reduction in patient anxiety. Alder added, wryly, 'We even labeled the cables 'Gillikin Standard' and 'Quadling Adapter.' Saves a lot of confused staring at the ports.'

In the Emerald City, the grant assisted a small business facing a supply-chain disruption. Ruth Fenwick, cobbler at Fenwick's on Lantern Row, relies on specialty boot heels from Quadling tanneries. When a shipment was delayed by four days, it threatened to push back her entire repair schedule. Using grant funds allocated for communication and customer retention, Fenwick notified her regular customers of the delay via postcard. More significantly, she included an offer for a discount on their next repair as an apology for the inconvenience. 'Postage isn't cheap,' Fenwick stated simply. 'But neither is losing a customer who thinks you don't respect their time. The discount comes out of my margin, but the grant covered the telling of it.' The action maintained client trust during an unpredictable delay.

Financially, the grant has been fully allocated. Expenditures fell into three categories: materials for the clinic charging station ($85), communication costs for the Lantern Row business continuity effort ($40 in postage and printing), and funds reserved for facilitating the South Meadow cooperative's planning sessions ($75, covering meeting space and materials). The total spent matches the $200 grant award.

In summary, the Meadow Mutual Aid network has applied these resources to concrete, localized interventions. One project is complete (the clinic charging station), one reactive measure was successfully executed (the customer communication), and one cooperative model is being developed (the inspection credit). All activities align with the fund's goal of fostering practical, community-driven support. We extend our thanks for the opportunity to undertake this work.

Respectfully submitted,
Rowan Bell, on behalf of the Meadow Mutual Aid Network

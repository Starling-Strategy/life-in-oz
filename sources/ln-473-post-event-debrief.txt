Post-event debrief: Gillikin Growers quarterly meet-up

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Rowan Bell. Gillikin Growers quarterly meet-up, May 2026. Notes from the dinner at the Grange Hall in Violet Hills. Attendance: Silas Hazelmere, Dacey Barleybourne, Bram Poppyworth, Ren Cinderholt, and about two dozen other members.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Main topics: season prep, equipment, quality control. The potluck was heavy on root vegetables and rye bread, appropriately. Key points from members:

Silas Hazelmere:
Silas said the scales for the Giant Pumpkin Contest are finally sorted. He got three large industrial ones from an old warehouse in the Emerald City, had them calibrated by a specialist there. Said he spent a whole afternoon checking each one with certified weights. 'No more arguing over whether it's 842 or 843 pounds. The reading's the reading.' He mentioned last year's second-place winner, who swore the first-place pumpkin was 'hollowed out and filled with sand,' was the main reason he pushed for this. Seems happy it's done.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Members seemed relieved. A couple joked about bringing their own counterweights next year, but Silas just shook his head, weary but firm. Said the scales will be stored at the Reed Flats market shed.

Dacey Barleybourne:
Dacey talked about the millstones at Gillikin Falls. Said the flour was getting gritty, customers were complaining. She tracked down an older miller, retired, who came in and showed her how to dress the stones properly. Said it took two full days. 'You listen to the sound it makes. A good stone has a certain song.' Flour quality is back up. She was matter-of-fact about it, but you could tell she was proud of the fix.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Bram, during the roundtable on season starters, brought up his idea for sharing heat mats. He said the early spring cold snap this year wiped out a lot of seedlings for smaller growers. His proposal: Gillikin Growers buys a pool of electric seedling mats, members sign up for them in two-week slots during propagation season. He had a rough calendar drawn on a napkin. Said it would be cheaper and more reliable than everyone trying to rig something up with lanterns and straw. It's just a plan; he's going to draft a proper proposal for the next meeting.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Ren, hearing about Dacey's millstone work, chimed in about quality control from the other end. He offered to run a test batch for a new farmers' cooperative that's trying rye. Said they're nervous about milling characteristics, don't want to commit a whole harvest if the yield is poor or the flour is off. Ren said his mill in Violet Hills could do a small batch, maybe fifty pounds, give them a report on extraction rate and ash content. He hasn't done it yet; he said he'll reach out to the co-op's lead next week to set it up.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Other business: a brief discussion about securing more straw mulch from the Timber Verge before the rains. No resolution, but Bram said he'd talk to the Repair Co-op contact. The group agreed the meet-up was useful; the informal setting after dinner let people get into the nitty-gritty. No open actions for me, but I should check in with Bram in a month about the heat mat proposal draft, and with Ren to see if the test milling happened.

Heath Pepperworth:
Quick update from Brickway Junction: we got the temporary sidewalk permit from the Wizard's Office. It’s for Tuesday and Thursday evenings, the four peak hours when the pantry line gets long enough to be a safety concern on the road shoulder. The permit’s good for the next two months. Nothing grand, but it means our neighbors can wait without worrying about the transit coaches, and the road crew doesn’t have to worry about us. Big relief.

Dilys Cinderham:
Heath, that’s excellent. A little bit of official flexibility makes everything smoother. Speaking of moving things smoothly… last week I ran a trial for Cinderham Cargo Bikes: delivering cut flowers from the Emerald City wholesale market to five suburban florists. Used insulated panniers with cold packs. Did the full run in the morning, before the day’s heat could wilt anything. The florists were pleased—said the blooms arrived in better shape than some of the van deliveries. No petals bruised from being jostled. We’re looking at scaling that up.

Teo Orchardwood:
Fascinating! The logistics of delicate cargo—it’s a language all its own. Dilys, your trial reminds me of something I’ve been mulling. I’d like to propose a free, informal workshop for small business owners on how to work effectively with translation services for your basic materials—menus, flyers, care instructions, that sort of thing. Not about becoming fluent, but about how to brief a translator so you get what you need, and how to handle updates without starting from scratch every time. Might be useful?

Dilys Cinderham:
Teo, yes. A hundred times yes. I’ve been staring at the draft of a cargo care sheet for our new Winkie Country clients. I know what I want it to say, but getting it from my head into clear Munchkin and Winkie dialect instructions feels like a bigger project than building the bike rack. A workshop would be brilliant.

Heath Pepperworth:
I’d be interested, too. Our pantry signage is only in Munchkin right now, and we get visitors from over the Quadling border sometimes. Even just knowing how to ask for a good translation of ‘welcome, take what you need’ would be a step up.

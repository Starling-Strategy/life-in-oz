Rowan Bell — community manager:
Held at the Chamber offices, 4:00pm, July 18th. Present: Verity Meadowford, Zeb Lanternham, Ezra Winkleley, Rowan Bell (notes).

Rowan Bell — community manager:
General mood was positive, focused on practical improvements and local supply chains. Some good momentum from the last quarter.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Verity shared an update on her stationery sourcing. She recently arranged for a small paper mill in the Quadling Country to produce a specific batch of cream-laid sheets for the local poetry press, The Gilt Quill. The paper has a discreet watermark of a quill in the corner. She brought a sample – it’s lovely, substantial but not stiff. She said the press was thrilled; it made their limited-edition run feel truly special and locally rooted. She mentioned the mill owner was grateful for the consistent order.

Verity Meadowford:
It’s not just about the paper, it’s about the connection. Now when someone in the city buys that book, they’re holding a piece of work from here to there. It feels right.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Zeb reported back on the new member welcome pack project. He’s done it: streamlined the whole thing. Gone is the three-inch binder that everyone said just gathered dust. The new version is a simple, elegant folder containing a one-page list of essential Chamber and city contacts, and a six-month calendar of key networking events and committee meetings. He had a prototype. It’s clean, easy to update, and fits in a desk drawer.

Zeb Lanternham:
Feedback from the three newest members has been unanimously positive. They appreciated not being handed what one called ‘a homework assignment.’ It’s about accessibility, not volume.

Rowan Bell — community manager:
Ezra floated a new proposal for Chamber-facilitated services. He’s suggesting the Chamber coordinate a quarterly secure-shredding service pickup for member businesses that handle sensitive paper archives – client records, old contracts, that sort of thing. The idea is to get a bulk rate from a reputable disposal firm and have a designated morning where a truck comes to the Chamber loading zone. Members could sign up and drop boxes.

Ezra Winkleley:
Many of my smaller clients don’t generate enough volume for a dedicated service contract, but they have a legal duty to dispose of certain documents properly. A pooled quarterly service, vouched for by the Chamber, would solve a real pain point. I’m happy to draft a one-page outline of the proposal for circulation.
